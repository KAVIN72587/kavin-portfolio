The brief

The couple came to me eight months after the wedding, which happens more often than people expect. They had a full-length edit they never opened and wanted something short enough to actually send to relatives — the request was, more or less, "the version we'd put on WhatsApp".

They also wanted the complete ceremony kept somewhere, for the grandparents and for later. So the job was two deliverables from one set of cards, not a shorter version of the same thing.

Why the first edit failed

Nothing was technically wrong with it. It was assembled in the order the day happened — arrival, ceremony, meal, reception, exit — with a film song over the whole thing and every guest given a few seconds of screen time.

That structure treats a wedding as a schedule to document. But nobody remembers a wedding as a schedule, and a fifty-minute file has no moment where anyone would choose to press play a second time.

Editing rule that came out of it

Chronology is how the day happened. Feeling is how it is remembered. Select for the second and let the order follow.

Finding the spine

There was no lapel mic and no recorder, so usable speech had to be found in camera audio recorded across a noisy hall. I went through all fourteen hours once, marking anything audible rather than anything well-shot.

What survived was about ninety seconds of clean material in total: part of the bride's father speaking at the reception, two lines from the ceremony, and a laugh from an aunt off-camera. That ninety seconds became the structure, and the pictures were then chosen to sit against it.

The best shot in fourteen hours was the mother watching from the third row, and it was in nobody's shot list.

Outcome

A 4:10 highlight film, a 62-minute full ceremony edit for the family, and a 40-second vertical teaser. The couple sent the short film to relatives the same week — which the fifty-minute version had never been, and is the only measure that matters here.

The one thing I would change is not mine to fix: with a single recorder on the officiant and one on the reception mic, there would have been ten minutes of usable speech instead of ninety seconds. That is worth telling any couple still planning a wedding.
