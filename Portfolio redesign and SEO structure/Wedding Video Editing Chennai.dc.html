How I cut a wedding

Most wedding edits are built chronologically — arrival, ceremony, meal, reception — which is how the day happened but not how anyone remembers it. I select for feeling instead, then let the order follow.

The mother watching from the third row is usually a better shot than the ceremony itself.

  1. STEP 01

    Watch everything

    All of it, once, marking the moments that hold — not the ones on the shot list.

  2. STEP 02

    Find the audio spine

    A vow, a speech line, a laugh. The film is built on that, not on the music.

  3. STEP 03

    Rough cut for approval

    Structure and pacing first, before time goes into colour and titles.

  4. STEP 04

    Grade, mix, deliver

    Colour matched across cameras, audio cleaned, exports for TV and for phones.

What footage I need from you

I edit; I don't shoot. If your videographer supplies these, the edit goes smoothly.

  • All camera cards, unedited

    Original files, not a pre-trimmed selection. Compressed re-exports lose grading headroom.

  • Separate audio, if recorded

    A lapel or recorder track transforms the vows section. Camera-mic audio rarely survives a hall.

  • Names and correct spellings

    For titles. A misspelt name in a wedding film is the one mistake nobody forgives.

  • Anyone who must appear

    Grandparents, a relative who travelled far. Tell me before the cut, not after.

  • A music direction

    A reference track or a mood. I supply licensed music matched to it.

  • A hard disk, ideally

    For a full wedding, couriering a drive across Chennai beats uploading half a terabyte.
