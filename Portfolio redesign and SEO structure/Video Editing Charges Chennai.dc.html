What actually moves the price

Not the length of the finished video. A 60-second reel can cost more than a ten-minute podcast edit, and these four things are why.

  1. FACTOR 01

    Footage in, not video out

    Fourteen hours of wedding footage cut to four minutes takes far longer than a ten-minute podcast from two clean camera files. The editor's time goes into watching and selecting, and that scales with what you send, not what comes out.

  2. FACTOR 02

    Cut density

    A fast reel has a cut every second and a half. A corporate interview has one every eight. Same duration, five times the decisions — this is why short-form is not automatically cheaper.

  3. FACTOR 03

    Graphics and motion

    Lower thirds and captions are part of the base rate. Animated charts, product callouts and anything built in After Effects is separate, because it is design work rather than editing.

  4. FACTOR 04

    How many people approve it

    One decision-maker means two revision rounds. A committee means six, half of them contradictory. Corporate rates are higher partly because of this, and a single named approver genuinely lowers your cost.

What a ₹800 quote leaves out

Very low quotes are usually real prices for a smaller job than you think you're buying. Check for these before comparing.

  • Revisions billed separately

    The first cut is cheap; every change after costs. Two rounds are included in my rates.

  • Copyrighted music

    A film song gets your video claimed or muted within a day. Licensed tracks are included in mine.

  • No colour or audio work

    Clips assembled in order with no grading or level matching. It shows immediately.

  • One aspect ratio only

    A horizontal export squeezed into a vertical frame rather than reframed properly.

  • Auto-generated captions

    Unchecked machine captions mangle names and Tamil words. Mine are corrected by hand.

  • Template edits

    The same preset applied to everyone's footage. Cheap, fast, and identical to fifty other channels.
