Freelancer or agency in Chennai?

I'm the freelancer, so read this knowing that. I've also worked alongside agencies enough to know exactly which jobs they do better — and there's a section below saying so plainly. Use the table to work out which side your project sits on.

Freelancer
Agency

Cost

You pay for design hours and little else. A logo at ₹8,000, a five-page site at ₹20,000.

The same brief typically lands two to four times higher, because the quote carries salaries, office and account management.

Who does the work

The person you spoke to. What you saw in the portfolio is what you get.

Often a junior, supervised. The senior who pitched may not touch the file.

Speed of feedback

You message me, I reply. Changes usually happen the same day.

Feedback goes through an account manager, into a queue, back out. Days, not hours.

Capacity

One project at a time gets full attention; three at once means a queue. Big parallel launches are a real limit.

Several tracks can run simultaneously. This is the honest advantage.

Range of services

Design, web and video from one desk. No media buying, no photography crew, no PR.

Strategy, media, production and design under one roof, with the coordination that needs.

Continuity risk

If I'm ill or travelling, your work waits. Mitigated by source files always being with you.

Someone covers. Continuity is what you're partly paying for.

Paperwork

Simple invoice, 50% advance, GST where applicable.

Contracts, vendor registration and insurance — heavier, but required by some procurement teams.

Knowing the work

I remember every file I made for you, because there are not thousands of them.

Institutional memory lives in a system, which survives staff changes better than a freelancer's absence.

When you should hire the agency

Four situations where I'd tell you to go elsewhere, because pretending otherwise wastes your money and my reputation.

  • Several things at once, on the same date

    A launch needing a film crew, a photoshoot, packaging and a site in the same four weeks needs parallel teams. I work in sequence.

  • You need media buying and strategy

    I design and edit. I don't plan campaigns, buy ad space or run performance marketing, and I won't pretend to.

  • Procurement requires a vendor panel

    Larger companies often need registered vendors, insurance cover and a legal entity on the invoice. That's a fair requirement and an agency meets it.

  • The work must never pause

    If a daily output can't stop when one person is ill or travelling, you need a team with cover built in.

Where the money actually goes

An agency quote isn't inflated for no reason — it carries costs I don't have. Whether that's worth paying depends entirely on your project.

The questions worth asking either way

Can I see who will actually do the work?+

Ask for the name of the person, not the studio, and look at their individual portfolio. With a freelancer that question answers itself; with an agency it is worth asking before you sign.

What happens if we stop working together?+

Get confirmation in writing that source files transfer on final payment, from whoever you hire. Some agencies retain working files by default and charge to release them.

How do I compare two very different quotes?+

Compare deliverables line by line, not headline prices. Number of concepts, revision rounds, file formats and who owns the result explain most of the gap.

Is a cheaper quote a warning sign?+

Only if the scope is vague. A low number attached to a clear list of deliverables is just lower overheads. A low number with no list is a problem regardless of who quoted it.

Can a freelancer handle a company of our size?+

Depends on the volume, not the company. A 200-person firm needing one brochure a month is a fine fit. A 20-person startup shipping daily creative is not.

Still not sure which you need?

Describe the project. If it's an agency job, I'll say so — and I know a few good ones in the city.
