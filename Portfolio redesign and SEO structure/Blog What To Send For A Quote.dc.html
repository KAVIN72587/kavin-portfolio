What to send a designer before asking for a quote

Most enquiries I get say "how much for a logo?" and nothing else. The honest answer is a range so wide it's useless. Five pieces of information turn that into a real number on the first reply.

This is not designers being difficult. The same word covers jobs that differ by a factor of ten — "a logo" might mean one mark for a WhatsApp profile, or a full identity going onto packaging, uniforms and four branch signboards. Until the designer knows which, any number they give you is a guess they will later have to revise, which is where the awkward conversations come from.

Send these five things and you will get a fixed price, a date, and a much better sense of whether this person is any good.

On sharing your budget

Clients hold this back because they assume the quote will expand to fill it. Some will. But a designer who knows you have ₹15,000 can tell you what ₹15,000 genuinely buys, or say plainly that your brief needs ₹40,000 and let you decide — before either of you spends a week finding out.

Withholding the budget does not get you a lower price. It gets you three rounds of email.

A range is fine. "Somewhere around ten to fifteen thousand" is all anyone needs to scope sensibly.
