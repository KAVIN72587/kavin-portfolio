The shelf test

Before you approve anything, I print the front panels at actual size, mount them on dummy packs and stand them beside your real competitors bought from a shop nearby. Then I photograph the row from two metres and look at it as a phone thumbnail.

Concepts that looked strongest in a presentation routinely vanish in that row. Finding out at that stage costs an afternoon. Finding out after a print run costs the run.

The compliance side

Food and cosmetic packs carry mandatory declarations. Fitting them without wrecking the design is most of the craft.

  • FSSAI block

    Licence number, veg or non-veg mark, and the declarations set at legible size, not hidden in 4pt type.

  • Net weight & MRP

    Placed where legal metrology expects them, with room for the batch and date stamp your filler adds later.

  • Ingredients & allergens

    Set as a proper text block that stays readable as the list grows in a later revision.

  • Barcode

    At scannable size with quiet zones intact, on a flat area away from seals and gussets.

  • Bilingual copy

    Tamil and English set at matched weight, planned into the layout rather than squeezed in at the end.

  • Dieline accuracy

    Drawn to your converter's template so nothing important lands on a fold, a seal or the tear notch.
