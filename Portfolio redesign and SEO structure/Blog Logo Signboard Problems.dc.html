Why your logo looks wrong on the signboard

The file you approved on a laptop and the thing bolted above your shutter are made by completely different processes. Here is where the gap opens up, and what to check before the fabricator starts cutting.

screen file vs fabricated sign — drop image

A logo on screen is made of light on a perfectly flat surface at a size you control. A signboard is made of cut vinyl, routed acrylic, painted metal or LED channel letters, viewed from twenty feet away at an angle, in daylight and then at night. Nothing about the first situation guarantees the second.

Most of the disappointment I see is not the fabricator's fault and not the designer's taste. It is a handful of specific, predictable failures. Each one is cheap to prevent in the file and expensive to fix in aluminium.

The test that catches almost everything

Print the logo at 2 inches wide on ordinary paper, in black only. Tape it to a wall and walk back four metres. If you cannot tell what it says, or two shapes have merged into one, the signboard will have the same problem at ten times the size — distance scales the flaw, it does not remove it.

If it survives a black-and-white paper print at 2 inches, it will survive the shutter.

What to send your fabricator

Four things, and the job usually comes back right the first time.

  • Vector artwork, fonts outlined

    An AI, EPS or PDF they can scale and cut from. A PNG at any resolution is not usable for routing or vinyl.

  • A single-colour version

    Solid black, no gradients or effects. This is what most signage methods can actually reproduce.

  • Named colour references

    Pantone or an automotive paint code, not a screen hex value. Hex means nothing to someone mixing paint.

  • The board dimensions

    Width and height of the actual space. Artwork laid out for a square board looks stranded on a wide one.

If your logo fails several of the checks above, the fix is usually a signage variant rather than a rebrand — a simplified version of the same mark, drawn for cutting. That is an afternoon's work, not a new identity.
