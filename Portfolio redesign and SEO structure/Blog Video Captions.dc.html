Most people watch your video with the sound off

On a bus, in an office, in a lobby, in bed at midnight next to someone asleep. The voiceover you paid for is not being heard, which makes captions the primary channel rather than a courtesy.

Once you accept that, several editing decisions change. The information that matters has to appear on screen, not just in the audio. A number said aloud and never shown is a number most of your audience never receives.

Why auto-captions are not enough

Automatic captions have got remarkably good at general English. They still fail predictably on exactly the words that matter most in a business video.

Burnt in or a separate file?

Burnt-in captions are part of the picture. They always appear, styled the way you designed them, on every platform including WhatsApp forwards. The cost is that they cannot be turned off or translated later.

A separate subtitle file can be toggled, searched by the platform, and swapped for another language. But the viewer has to turn it on, and most never do — which defeats the purpose for social video.

Burn them in for social. Supply a file as well for YouTube and your website.

Doing both is barely extra work once the transcript is corrected, and it means search engines can read what your video says. For a Tamil and English audience it also gives you the option of burnt-in Tamil with an English file on top, or the reverse.

Beyond captions

Designing for silent viewing goes further than transcribing speech. Prices, dates, phone numbers and offers belong on screen as graphics regardless of whether they are also spoken. A product demo needs visible labels rather than a narrator describing what you are looking at.

The test is simple, and worth running before you approve any edit: mute it and watch the whole thing. If you cannot follow what the video is telling you, neither can most of your audience.
