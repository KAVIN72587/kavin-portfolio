Cut it to the point

The most valuable thing an editor does on corporate work is remove material the company is attached to. Founder's history, every department mentioned, the full award list — all of it costs you the viewer before the point arrives.

Nobody has ever complained that a company video was too short.

  • One video, one job

    A film that introduces the company, sells the product and recruits staff does none of the three. Two shorter videos beat one long one.

  • The first eight seconds

    Logo animations belong at the end. Open on the thing that makes someone stay.

  • Watched without sound

    On LinkedIn and in a lobby, audio is off. Subtitles are burnt in and key numbers appear on screen.

Working with a marketing team

  • Brand kit up front

    Fonts, colours and logo files at the start. Lower thirds and titles then match your other material exactly.

  • One approver

    Feedback consolidated by one person. Six people sending contradictory notes is what makes edits take three weeks.

  • Timecoded feedback

    Review links where comments attach to the frame. Far faster than a document describing a moment in words.

  • NDA and confidentiality

    Signed on request before any footage moves. Some work never appears in my portfolio, which is normal and fine.

  • Invoicing and GST

    Proper invoices with PO references, matched to your finance team's process and payment cycle.

  • Source files on request

    Project files handed over if your team needs to re-version later. No holding your footage hostage.
