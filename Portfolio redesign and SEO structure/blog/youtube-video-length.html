The right length for your YouTube videos

"Should our videos be under five minutes or over ten?" There is a lot of advice online claiming a perfect length for YouTube. The honest answer is that the right length is as long as the video stays useful and engaging, and not a second longer. What matters is how much of it people actually watch.

Here is why retention matters more than length, typical lengths that work for different kinds of business videos, and how editing makes a video feel the right length.

01

Retention beats length

YouTube's systems look at how viewers respond to a video, including how long they watch and whether they seem satisfied. A tight six-minute video that most viewers watch to the end usually performs better than a padded fifteen-minute video most people abandon halfway. Length is only an advantage when the extra minutes hold attention.

YouTube Studio shows audience retention for each video: a graph of how many viewers are still watching at each moment. It is the most useful tool for deciding length.

02

Tutorials and how-to videos

Viewers searching for a specific answer want it quickly. Many good how-to videos are three to ten minutes. Answer the question early, then add depth. Chapters help viewers jump to the part they need. If a topic genuinely needs twenty minutes, it can work, but every minute must be useful.

03

Explainers and service videos

A video explaining what your business does, or how a service works, is often two to five minutes. Viewers are evaluating you, and attention is limited. Focus on the problem you solve, how it works and what to do next.

These videos often get embedded on your website too, where visitors are even less patient. A shorter cut of under two minutes for the website, linking to the full version on YouTube, can serve both audiences.

04

Long-form expertise and interviews

Podcasts, interviews, webinars and in-depth discussions can run thirty minutes to over an hour, because their audience chooses them deliberately. Even so, editing matters: remove long pauses, tangents and setup chatter. Clips from these long videos make good Shorts that lead viewers back to the full episode.

05

Shorts

Shorts are vertical videos up to the platform's current length limit. They work best when they deliver one idea quickly. They are useful for reaching new viewers and leading them to your longer videos, but they are a separate format, not a shortened version of everything.

06

How editing controls length

Most raw recordings contain the same content as the final video plus a lot of padding: repeated points, slow introductions, long pauses, setup and filler. Good editing removes that without losing substance. A twenty-minute recording often becomes a better ten-minute video. Pacing techniques, such as cutting to B-roll, on-screen text and changing angles, also make the same length feel shorter.

07

Do not pad for length

Some channels stretch videos to reach a certain duration, believing longer is always better. Viewers notice, and retention suffers. If a topic is covered in seven minutes, publish seven minutes. If you have more to say, make another video.

08

Let your own data decide

After ten or fifteen videos, look at YouTube Studio for patterns. Which lengths have the best average percentage viewed? Where do viewers leave, in the introduction, in a particular section, or near the end? Adjust the next videos accordingly. Your own audience's behaviour is more reliable than any general rule about ideal length, and it will keep changing as your channel grows.

Watch your retention graph

Look for the moments when viewers drop off: a slow introduction, a long tangent, a repeated point. Those are the parts to cut or tighten in the next video. Over time, your own data tells you the right length for your audience better than any rule.

As long as it is useful, and not a second longer.

A good editor uses that retention data too, shaping future edits around what your viewers actually watch.

Typical lengths that work

  1. How-to and tutorial. Three to ten minutes, answer early, chapters.
  2. Business or service explainer. Two to five minutes.
  3. Case study or testimonial. One to three minutes.
  4. Interview or podcast. Thirty minutes plus, tightly edited, with Shorts clips.
  5. Shorts. Under a minute in most cases, one idea.

These are starting points. Retention data from your own channel should guide adjustments after the first ten or so videos.

Quick answers

What is the best length for a YouTube video?

There is no single best length. Videos should be as long as they stay useful and engaging. Retention, how much people watch, matters more than duration.

Are longer YouTube videos better for ranking?

Only if viewers keep watching. A tight video with strong retention often performs better than a longer video that viewers abandon.

How long should a business explainer video be on YouTube?

Usually two to five minutes, focusing on the problem, how you solve it and the next step.
