Should your website be in Tamil too?

Many Chennai businesses serve customers who are more comfortable in Tamil. So a natural question comes up when building a website: should it be in Tamil, English or both? The answer depends on who searches for you, how, and whether you can keep two versions up to date.

Here is who a Tamil version genuinely helps, how to set up a bilingual website properly, and the common mistakes that make a second language more trouble than it is worth.

01

Who searches in Tamil

Most people in Chennai search Google in English or Tanglish, even when they prefer Tamil in conversation. But some customers, especially older people, people outside the metro and customers of traditional businesses, are far more comfortable reading Tamil. A temple trust, a Siddha clinic, an agricultural supplier, a local school or a government-facing service may reach many more people with Tamil content.

Ask your customers. Look at the language of the WhatsApp messages and calls you get. That is better evidence than any assumption.

02

Option 1: English site, Tamil touches

For many businesses, the practical choice is an English website with Tamil where it matters: a Tamil tagline, key service names in both languages, a Tamil call-to-action such as "WhatsApp-இல் தொடர்பு கொள்ளுங்கள்", and perhaps a single page in Tamil summarising what you do. It costs little, adds warmth, and avoids maintaining two full sites.

Make sure your business name, address and phone number appear exactly the same way in both languages wherever they are used, so customers and Google recognise them as the same business.

03

Option 2: a full Tamil version

If a large share of your customers prefer Tamil, build a proper second version: each page translated, with its own address such as /ta/ for Tamil pages, a visible language switch, and correct technical tags so Google shows the right version to the right searcher. This is effectively a second set of pages to build, test and maintain, not a toggle switch.

Avoid automatic translation widgets. Machine-translated Tamil often reads awkwardly and can change meanings; customers notice.

04

Translation quality matters

Tamil written for a website should be natural, modern and clear, not overly formal or literal. A literal translation of English marketing copy often sounds stiff. Ideally, someone who writes Tamil well adapts the content rather than translating word for word. Have a native speaker who knows your customers read every page before launch.

05

Fonts and layout

Tamil text needs a well-designed Tamil web font that renders correctly on all phones and supports all characters. Tamil words are often longer than their English equivalents, so headings, buttons and menus need room to grow. A layout designed only for English often breaks when Tamil is added, so plan for both from the start.

06

What it does for search

A proper Tamil version can appear in searches made in Tamil, where there is often far less competition than in English. For local services with Tamil-speaking customers, this can be a real advantage. But a thin, outdated Tamil version helps nobody. Google ranks useful pages, in any language.

07

The maintenance question

Every update now happens twice: new prices, new services, new photos, new timings. If only the English site is updated, the Tamil one quietly becomes wrong, which is worse than not having it. Before building a bilingual site, decide who will update the Tamil pages and how quickly. If the answer is unclear, start with Option 1.

Start with what customers actually need

A bilingual website is a good investment when a meaningful share of your customers prefer Tamil and you can keep both versions current. For most businesses, Tamil touches on an English site give most of the benefit at a fraction of the effort.

Two languages means two websites to keep up to date.

Whichever option you choose, design the layout and choose fonts with both languages in mind, so adding more Tamil later does not mean rebuilding.

Deciding quickly

  1. Most enquiries in English or Tanglish? English site with Tamil touches.
  2. Many customers prefer Tamil? A full Tamil version, properly translated.
  3. Nobody to maintain the Tamil pages? Do not build them yet.
  4. Print materials already in Tamil? Match the website to them for consistency.

The same thinking applies to social media and printed materials. Customers should meet the same language and tone everywhere they encounter your business.

Quick answers

Should my Chennai business website be in Tamil?

If many of your customers prefer reading Tamil, a Tamil version helps. If most search and enquire in English or Tanglish, an English site with Tamil touches is usually enough.

Can I use Google Translate for a Tamil website?

It is not recommended. Machine-translated Tamil often reads awkwardly and can change meanings. Have the content adapted by someone who writes Tamil well.

Does a Tamil version help with Google rankings?

A proper Tamil version can rank for Tamil searches, where competition is often lower. It needs correct technical setup and genuinely useful, maintained content.
