Stall graphics that pull visitors in

Trade fairs and exhibitions in Chennai, from industrial expos to wedding fairs, education fairs and food festivals, bring thousands of potential customers into one hall. Your stall competes with hundreds of others for a few seconds of attention as visitors walk past. Graphics that are cluttered, too small or unclear get ignored, however good the product is.

Here is how to plan and design exhibition stall graphics that catch attention from the aisle, explain what you do quickly and help your team start conversations.

01

Start with one clear message

Decide what visitors should understand in three seconds: who you are and what you offer. "Precision CNC components for automotive" or "Custom wedding sarees in pure silk" is far more useful than a slogan that needs explanation.

Everything on the stall should support that message.

02

The backdrop

The back wall is the largest graphic and is visible from across the aisle. Put your logo and main message high up, above head height, so they remain visible when people are standing in front. Use one strong image and very little text.

Avoid filling the backdrop with product lists and contact details. Those belong on brochures, counters and screens.

03

The fascia and side panels

Many shell scheme stalls have a fascia board with your company name and stall number. Make sure the name is spelled correctly and matches your brand. Side panels, visible to people approaching along the aisle, can carry your message or a key product image.

Get the organiser's stall specifications early: panel sizes, fascia text rules, hanging restrictions and deadlines for graphics.

04

Standees, counters and displays

Roll-up standees at the stall entrance can highlight a specific product, offer or demo. A branded counter or table front gives your team a place to talk and collect leads. Product displays should be well lit and easy to see from the aisle.

Screens showing short product videos or demos attract attention, especially if they move and have captions, since halls are noisy.

05

Plan with the fabricator

Stall fabricators need exact graphic sizes, materials and deadlines. Ask them for templates for every panel and confirm how graphics will be fixed. Provide print-ready files at the correct size with the bleed they need. Late or wrongly sized files are the most common cause of stall problems on setup day.

06

Handouts and lead capture

Prepare brochures, catalogues or simple leaflets that match your stall graphics. A QR code on the counter linking to your WhatsApp, catalogue PDF or a lead form helps you capture interest even when your team is busy. Consider a small giveaway or offer for visitors who share their details.

07

Reuse what you can

If you attend several exhibitions a year, design modular graphics that can be reused: a backdrop that fits different stall sizes, standees that work anywhere and panels that can be swapped for specific events. It saves cost and keeps your presence consistent.

Be understood from the aisle

Visitors decide whether to stop in a few seconds, from a few metres away. A clear message placed high on the backdrop, strong images, well-lit products and a welcoming counter do more than a stall packed with text. Make it easy to understand, and your team can do the rest.

If they can't tell what you do from the aisle, they'll keep walking.

After each event, note which graphics drew questions and which were ignored. Use that to improve the next stall.

Exhibition graphics checklist

  1. Message. Who you are and what you offer, in one line.
  2. Backdrop. Logo and message above head height, one strong image.
  3. Fascia. Company name spelled correctly.
  4. Standees and counter. Product highlights, offers, lead capture.
  5. Files. Exact sizes and bleed from the fabricator.
  6. Handouts. Matching brochures and QR codes.

Send graphics to the fabricator well before the deadline. Exhibition halls rarely allow last-minute changes on setup day.

Brief your team on the stall message too. When everyone at the stall can explain the business in the same simple sentence as the backdrop, visitors get a consistent story from the first glance to the final conversation. Design attracts people to the stall; a clear, confident team turns that attention into leads.

Budget for the whole stall, not just the space rental. Graphics, fabrication, lighting, screens, handouts and giveaways all add up, and planning them together avoids last-minute compromises on the most visible elements, especially the backdrop and fascia that every visitor sees first.

Quick answers

What graphics does an exhibition stall need?

A backdrop, fascia board, side panels, standees, counter graphics, product displays and matching handouts, depending on stall size and type.

Where should the logo go on an exhibition backdrop?

High up, above head height, so it stays visible when people are standing in front of the stall.

How early should exhibition graphics be prepared?

Several weeks before the event. Fabricators need final files well ahead of setup day, and organisers often have strict deadlines.
