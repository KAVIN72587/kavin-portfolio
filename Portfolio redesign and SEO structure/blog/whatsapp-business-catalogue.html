A WhatsApp catalogue that sells in chat

For many small businesses in Chennai, the real shop window is not a website or Instagram. It is WhatsApp. Customers ask "what do you have?" and the owner sends a stream of random photos, some blurry, some with old prices, some forwarded from suppliers. The customer scrolls, gets confused and often stops replying.

The WhatsApp Business catalogue feature lets you show products neatly inside the app, with photos, prices and descriptions. Set up well, it saves time on every enquiry and makes your business look organised. Here is how to make it work.

01

Start with good product photos

Photos sell more than anything else in a catalogue. Use clear, well-lit photos of the actual product on a plain background. Natural daylight near a window works well. Keep the product centred with space around it, because WhatsApp shows images as squares in the catalogue grid.

Take photos in the same style for every product: same background, same angle, same lighting. A consistent catalogue looks professional, even if the photos are taken on a phone.

02

Image size and shape

Square images work best in the catalogue, at least 1080 × 1080 pixels. Keep the product in the centre so nothing important is cut off when WhatsApp crops thumbnails. You can add several images per product: front, back, detail, in use.

Avoid adding lots of text to product photos. Prices and descriptions belong in the catalogue fields, where they can be updated easily.

03

Clear names, prices and descriptions

Use product names customers would search or ask for: "Cotton saree, maroon, zari border" is more useful than "Product 23". Add the price if you can. Many customers will not ask, and an empty price field often means a lost sale.

Keep descriptions short and practical: size, material, colours available, what is included and delivery information. Add a product code if your team uses one, so orders are easy to match.

Update prices and stock regularly. A customer who chooses an item and is told it is unavailable or now costs more is unlikely to order again.

04

Organise into collections

Group products into collections such as new arrivals, bestsellers, sarees, kurtis, gift boxes or price ranges. Collections make large catalogues easy to browse and let you send customers straight to the right section.

05

Design a cover and profile

Your WhatsApp Business profile picture should be your logo icon, clear in a small circle. The business description, address, hours and website should be complete. A simple branded cover image or welcome message with your logo and a short line about what you sell helps first-time customers trust you.

If you send catalogue links in broadcasts, a branded image announcing new arrivals or offers, in your usual style, makes the message feel official rather than random.

06

Use it in every conversation

Once your catalogue is ready, share the catalogue or a specific item link instead of sending photos one by one. Pin it in your status, mention it in your Instagram bio and print a QR code linking to your WhatsApp on bills and packaging.

Treat WhatsApp like a real shop window

If most of your sales start in WhatsApp chats, your catalogue deserves the same care as a shop display. Clear photos, accurate prices and organised collections make customers more confident, cut down repetitive questions and help them order faster.

A tidy catalogue answers questions before customers ask them.

Review the catalogue every week or two. Remove sold-out items, add new ones and refresh your top collection so returning customers see something new.

WhatsApp catalogue checklist

  1. Photos. Square, clear, consistent background and light.
  2. Names. What customers would actually call the product.
  3. Prices. Filled in and kept up to date.
  4. Details. Size, material, colours, delivery.
  5. Collections. Grouped for easy browsing.
  6. Profile. Logo icon, description, hours, address, website.

If you sell many products, a short photo session to shoot everything consistently is worth more than any filter or design trick.

For businesses with frequent new stock, set aside a fixed time each week to photograph and add items. It turns catalogue updates from a chore into a routine, and regular customers learn that there is always something new to look at.

Finally, reply quickly. A beautiful catalogue loses its value if enquiries wait hours for an answer. Quick replies, saved answers for common questions and a clear note about delivery areas and payment methods turn browsing customers into buyers. The catalogue gets their attention; your response closes the sale.

Quick answers

What size should WhatsApp catalogue images be?

Square images of at least 1080 × 1080 pixels work well, with the product centred so it is not cropped in thumbnails.

Should I add prices to my WhatsApp catalogue?

Yes, if possible. Clear prices reduce back-and-forth messages, and many customers will not ask if the price is missing.

How many products can I add to a WhatsApp Business catalogue?

WhatsApp allows a large number of products. Organise them into collections so customers can browse easily.
