Teaser, highlight or full film?

Wedding packages list three videos that sound similar: a teaser, a highlight film and a full film. They are not long, longer and longest versions of the same thing. Each one is made for a different audience and a different moment.

Knowing the difference helps you choose what to pay for, set fair expectations about delivery, and brief your editor properly. Here is what each one is, roughly how long it runs, and who it is really for.

01

The teaser: one minute, for everyone, right away

A teaser is usually under a minute, often a vertical cut for Instagram. It is the trailer: the best few moments set to music, with no attempt to tell the whole story. Its job is to be posted while everyone is still talking about the wedding — within days, not weeks.

Because it is short and uses only the strongest shots, it can be turned around fast. I usually deliver teasers in two or three days from receiving the footage.

Who watches it: friends, relatives and followers, mostly on their phones, mostly once.

02

The highlight film: the one you will rewatch

A highlight film usually runs somewhere between three and ten minutes. It tells the story of the day — preparation, ceremony, emotions, celebration — cut to music, with vows, speeches or a few lines from parents woven in. It is the video you show people who could not come, and the one you watch on anniversaries.

This is where most of the editing effort goes: choosing moments from hours of footage, building a story, matching colour across cameras, and mixing music under real audio.

Who watches it: the couple and close family, many times, for years.

03

The full film: the record of the day

The full film, sometimes called the documentary or ceremony edit, covers the key events in order and in full: the muhurtham, the rituals, the speeches, the reception entry. It can run from forty minutes to two hours or more, depending on the ceremonies.

It is less about storytelling and more about completeness — trimming dead time, choosing the best camera angle for each moment, and making sure the audio is clear.

Who watches it: parents and grandparents, older relatives, and the couple when they want to relive a specific ritual.

Most couples need two, not three

If budget is tight, ask yourself who will watch what. Families who care about every ritual being recorded need the full film. Couples who mostly want something beautiful to share need the highlight. The teaser is the easiest to drop — a few clips posted from the photographer's account can do a similar job.

Pay for the video your family will actually watch, not the longest one on the list.

What you should not drop is the raw footage. Whatever you choose, make sure you receive and keep all of it. You can always have another edit made from it later.

Realistic delivery times

Editing time depends on hours of footage, number of cameras and the number of events. As a rough guide for editing only, counted from when the footage arrives:

  1. Teaser: two to three days.
  2. Highlight film plus full film together: two to three weeks.
  3. Multi-day weddings with several events: longer, agreed up front.

If a studio quotes months, the delay is usually the queue, not the edit. Wedding season fills every editor's calendar at once. Ask for a date in writing, and ask what happens if it slips.

What to tell your editor before they start

A wedding edit is personal. A few notes from you make the difference between a nice video and your video.

  1. The people who must appear. Grandparents, a sibling flying in from abroad, a friend who organised everything. Point them out with a clip name, because the editor has never met them.
  2. The moments that mattered to you. A line from a speech, a ritual your family cares about, the moment your father saw you. These belong in the highlight film.
  3. Music you love — and music you do not. Name two or three songs or styles. Remember that tracks you post publicly may need to be licensed or may get muted on some platforms.
  4. Language and subtitles. If vows or speeches are in Tamil and relatives abroad will watch, ask for English subtitles on those sections.
  5. Where each version will be shown. A phone, a TV at a family gathering, a projector at the reception. It changes how the film is exported.

Quick answers

How long is a wedding highlight video?

Usually between three and ten minutes. Under three can feel rushed for a full day; over ten starts to become a short film that people stop watching halfway through.

What is the difference between a wedding teaser and a trailer?

In practice they are the same thing: a short cut of the best moments, under a minute or two, made to share quickly. Some studios call a slightly longer, more story-led version a trailer.
