What hosting your website actually needs

Hosting is the rented computer your website lives on. Hosting companies sell it in a dozen confusing tiers, and most small businesses either overpay for power they never use or buy the cheapest plan and find out about its limits later.

Here is what hosting is, which type a typical small business site in India needs, and the handful of things worth checking before you pay. Prices and plan names change often, so treat the figures here as ranges and check the current offer before you buy.

01

Domain and hosting are two different things

The domain is your address (yourbusiness.in). Hosting is the space where the files live. You can buy both from the same company, but you do not have to, and they renew separately. People often discover this when the domain renews but the hosting lapses, or the other way round, and the site disappears.

Keep a note of where each one is bought, which email the account uses and when each renews. That one line in a notebook saves a lot of panic later.

02

Shared hosting is fine for most small businesses

Shared hosting means your site sits on a server with many others. It is cheap, usually ₹2,500 to ₹5,000 a year for a decent plan, and perfectly adequate for a five-page business site or a small WordPress site that gets a few thousand visits a month.

You do not need a VPS or a dedicated server for a brochure site. Those make sense for busy online stores, custom applications or sites with heavy traffic, and they come with more setup and more responsibility.

03

Managed WordPress hosting costs more, and sometimes earns it

Managed WordPress plans handle updates, caching, backups and security for you. They cost more than basic shared hosting. If nobody on your side will look after the site, and you are not paying someone for maintenance, the extra cost can be worth it simply because the boring jobs actually get done.

A static site (plain HTML, no WordPress) needs far less: some static hosts have generous free tiers, and there is almost nothing to update.

04

Server location matters less than people think

Hosts love to advertise Indian data centres. A server in India or Singapore can shave a little off loading time for local visitors, but image sizes, theme quality and caching make a much bigger difference. Do not pick a worse host only because its server is in Mumbai.

05

Read the renewal price, not the offer price

Most hosting is sold with a big first-term discount, often tied to a three or four year upfront payment. The renewal price can be two or three times higher. Compare plans on the renewal price, because that is what you will pay for most of the site's life.

Also check what is included at renewal: free domain offers and free email often apply only to the first year.

06

Backups are your insurance

A good host takes automatic daily backups and keeps several days of them. A cheap plan may take weekly backups, keep only one, or charge extra to restore it. Find out before something goes wrong, not after.

Even with host backups, keep your own copy. For a WordPress site, a backup plugin that saves to Google Drive or similar once a week is enough. For a static site, the files and a copy of the domain settings are all you need. If the host ever shuts your account, has an outage or loses data, you can be back online elsewhere the same day instead of starting from nothing.

Buy it in your own name

Whoever holds the hosting account controls the site. If a designer buys hosting on their account and later disappears, you may lose the site entirely, even though you paid for it. It happens more often than you would think.

Your business email, your card, your login. Then give the designer access.

A good designer will happily work inside an account you own. If someone insists on hosting your site on their own account, ask exactly how you would move it away if you needed to.

Before you pay for a hosting plan

  1. Renewal price. Write down what year two costs, not year one.
  2. Backups. Are they automatic, how often, and can you restore one yourself?
  3. SSL. A free certificate should be included. Do not pay separately unless the host gives no option.
  4. Support. Is there chat or phone support that answers within an hour?
  5. Limits. Number of sites, storage and email accounts on the plan you are buying.

Quick answers

Which hosting is best for a small business website in India?

For a typical five-page site or small WordPress site, a reputable shared hosting plan is enough. Choose on renewal price, automatic backups, free SSL and responsive support rather than on headline speed claims.

How much does web hosting cost per year in India?

A decent shared hosting plan for a small business site usually costs about ₹2,500 to ₹5,000 a year at renewal. First-year offers are often lower, so compare renewal prices.

Do I need a VPS for my business website?

Usually not. A VPS suits busy online stores, custom applications or high traffic. A brochure site or small WordPress site runs well on good shared hosting.
