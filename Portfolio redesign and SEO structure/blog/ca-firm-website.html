A website for your CA practice

Most clients find a chartered accountant through a referral, then look the firm up before calling. What they find in that search, or do not find, quietly decides whether the referral turns into a meeting.

Here is what a website for a CA firm or tax practice should cover, how to present services clearly, and why professional rules shape the tone. Chartered accountants in India are subject to ICAI guidelines on websites and advertising, so check the current guidelines, or have your website reviewed against them, before you publish.

01

Check the professional rules first

ICAI has issued guidelines on what members' websites may contain and how they may be presented. They have historically restricted things such as solicitation, comparative claims and certain kinds of client information. The rules have been revised over the years, so read the current version rather than relying on what a colleague did a decade ago.

In practice, this means a factual, professional tone: who you are, what services you provide, how to reach you. No boasts, no client lists without permission, no promises of outcomes.

If in doubt about a specific item, leave it out or check with ICAI's current guidance.

02

List services in plain language

Clients search for what they need, not for professional terminology. "GST registration and monthly returns", "income tax filing for salaried and business owners", "company incorporation and annual compliance", "audit" and "bookkeeping" are clearer than a single "assurance and advisory services" heading. A short description of each, including who it suits, helps the right clients self-select.

03

Show the people

Clients trust people, not logos. A team page with real photos, names, qualifications and areas of focus makes the firm feel approachable. Keep it factual: membership, years of practice and specialisations rather than adjectives.

04

Industries and client types

If most of your clients are small manufacturers, doctors, startups or NRIs, say so plainly. A business owner who sees their type of business listed feels understood. It also helps the site appear for more specific searches, provided the information stays factual rather than promotional.

The same applies to languages. If the partners handle clients in Tamil, Hindi or another language, mention it. For many small business owners, being able to discuss their tax position comfortably in their own language is a real reason to choose one firm over another.

05

Make contact simple

Phone, email, office address with a map, office hours and a short enquiry form asking what the enquiry is about. Many clients prefer WhatsApp for quick questions; if you offer it, say so. If you work with clients remotely across India or abroad, mention it, because many people assume they need someone nearby.

06

Security and confidentiality

Never ask for documents through a basic website form. Financial documents need a secure channel: a client portal, encrypted file sharing or your practice software. The website can explain how documents are shared securely, which reassures clients without handling sensitive files itself. Make sure the site uses HTTPS and has a simple privacy notice.

Trust is the whole job

An accountant's website is not there to impress with design. It is there to confirm that the firm is real, qualified, reachable and organised. Clean layout, clear services, real people and quick loading do that better than animations and stock images of handshakes.

Clear, factual and easy to contact beats clever.

A five-page site is usually enough: home, services, team, a resources or updates page if you will keep it current, and contact.

A sensible page structure

  1. Home. Firm name, location, a one-line summary of services, contact button.
  2. Services. Each service in plain language, with who it suits.
  3. Team. Photos, names, qualifications, focus areas.
  4. Updates. Only if someone will keep it current; due-date reminders are useful.
  5. Contact. Phone, email, map, hours, enquiry form, secure document process.

An updates page is the most tempting to add and the most often abandoned. If you do add one, short practical notes work best: a reminder of an upcoming filing deadline, a plain-language explanation of a change that affects your clients. Keep them factual, date every note, and remove or update anything that becomes outdated.

A page that is clearly maintained tells a visitor the firm is attentive. A page last updated three years ago tells them the opposite, so if nobody will own it, leave it out.

Quick answers

Can chartered accountants have a website in India?

Yes. ICAI permits members to have websites within its guidelines on content and presentation. Check the current ICAI guidelines before publishing, as they have been revised over time.

What should a CA firm website include?

Services described in plain language, the team with qualifications, client types or industries served, clear contact details with a map, and information on how documents are shared securely.

Should clients upload documents through the website?

Not through a basic contact form. Use a secure client portal or encrypted file-sharing service, and explain the process on the website.
