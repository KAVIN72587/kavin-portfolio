Monthly retainer or pay per video?

If you post videos regularly, editors will often offer two ways to pay: per video, as and when you need it, or a monthly retainer covering a set number of videos. The retainer is usually cheaper per video. It is not always the right choice.

Here is how both models work, the real savings, what a good retainer agreement includes, and how to choose based on how regularly you actually produce content. Figures below are from my own rate card; other editors will differ.

01

Per-video pricing

You pay for each video when you need it. It suits occasional projects: a corporate film, a launch video, a wedding, an event recap. There is no commitment, and you can use different editors for different jobs. The downside is that you join the editor's queue each time, and turnaround depends on their schedule.

You also brief from scratch each time, which takes longer for both sides.

02

Monthly retainers

You pay a fixed monthly fee for a set number of videos, such as four YouTube videos or twelve reels a month. The editor reserves capacity for you, often with fixed delivery days, and learns your style, so each video takes less briefing. Retainers are typically cheaper per video because the editor can plan their schedule.

03

What the savings look like

In my rate card, a YouTube video billed singly starts at ₹3,000, so four would be ₹12,000; on a retainer, four a month is ₹10,000. Twelve reels billed singly at ₹1,500 would be ₹18,000; the twelve-reel retainer is ₹12,000. Roughly a third cheaper per video, plus a predictable schedule.

Savings only count if you actually use the videos you pay for each month.

04

When a retainer makes sense

You post on a regular schedule, such as weekly YouTube or several reels a week. You have a steady supply of footage, or a routine for filming. You want a consistent style across videos. You value a fixed delivery day. Content is part of your ongoing marketing, not a one-off.

05

When per video is better

You only need videos occasionally. Your filming is irregular, such as around events, seasons or launches. You are testing whether video works for your business. You need different specialist editors for different kinds of projects. In these cases, a retainer can mean paying for capacity you do not use, which quietly cancels out the discount.

06

What to check in a retainer

How many videos and of what type and length. Turnaround and delivery days. Revision rounds per video. What happens to unused videos: do they roll over, or expire? Notice period to change or end the retainer. What happens if you send footage late. Whether thumbnails, captions, covers and different formats are included.

07

Start with a trial month

Before committing to a long retainer, try a single month, or a few per-video projects with the same editor. You will see whether the style, communication and turnaround suit you, and whether you can actually supply footage at the pace the retainer assumes.

08

Plan the month together

Retainers work best with a short monthly planning call or message: the topics or events coming up, when footage will arrive and the posting dates. The editor can then reserve time on the right days and suggest formats, such as a long video plus three clips from it. Without a plan, retainers drift into last-minute requests and unused videos, which wastes the advantage of a fixed slot.

Match the payment model to your rhythm

The cheapest model is the one that matches how you actually work. A retainer you use fully is good value. A retainer where half the videos go unused is more expensive than paying per video.

Retainers reward consistency. Per-video pricing rewards flexibility.

Look at the last three months: how many videos did you actually publish, and how regularly? That number is your guide.

Quick answers

Is a video editing retainer cheaper than paying per video?

Usually yes per video, often by around a third, plus you get a reserved slot. It is only cheaper overall if you use the videos you pay for each month.

What should a video editing retainer include?

The number and type of videos, turnaround and delivery days, revision rounds, what happens to unused videos, the notice period and which extras such as thumbnails and captions are included.

Should I start with a retainer?

It often helps to try one month, or a few per-video projects, first. That shows whether the editor suits you and whether you can supply footage regularly.
