Pongal creatives, planned in December

Pongal is the biggest festival of the year for many Tamil Nadu businesses: grocery stores, sweet shops, textile showrooms, home appliance dealers, jewellers and restaurants all see strong demand. But it arrives in mid-January, straight after Christmas and New Year, when designers, printers and business owners are all exhausted from the December rush. Creatives planned in the first week of January often go out late, or look rushed.

Here is how to plan Pongal offers and creatives in December, with themes that feel genuine, a list of what to design and a simple timeline.

01

Why plan in December

Customers start planning Pongal shopping in late December and early January: new clothes, kitchen items, gifts, groceries for the festival. Offers that appear in the first week of January catch that planning. Offers that appear two days before Bhogi compete with everyone else at once.

Printers are also busy with calendars, diaries and New Year jobs in December, so booking print work for Pongal early avoids delays.

02

Themes that feel authentic

Pongal has rich, recognisable visual traditions: the pongal pot boiling over, sugarcane, turmeric plants, kolam, the rising sun, cattle decorated for Mattu Pongal, and the colours of harvest. Used thoughtfully, these make creatives feel warm and local.

Avoid generic clip art that every business uses. Your own photos, such as your products arranged for the festival, your team in traditional clothes or your shop decorated with kolam, make posts feel personal and genuine.

03

Use Tamil well

Pongal greetings in Tamil connect strongly with local customers. Make sure the Tamil text is correct and well typeset in a font that matches your brand. Have it checked by a fluent reader. A bilingual layout, Tamil greeting with English offer details, works well for mixed audiences.

Check the exact Pongal and Bhogi dates for the coming year on the official calendar when you plan your posting schedule.

04

What to design

For a selling campaign: an announcement post, the main offer post, story versions, a reminder before Bhogi, greetings for Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal and Kaanum Pongal if relevant, and a thank-you post afterwards. Add a printed counter poster or standee, WhatsApp images and Google Business Profile posts.

For goodwill: a well-designed greeting post and story for Thai Pongal is enough.

05

The offer comes first

Decide the offer before briefing the designer: festival combos, discounts, gift-with-purchase, exchange offers or special Pongal products. Specific offers with end dates work better than vague "Pongal special offers" messages.

06

A simple timeline

Mid December: decide the offer and channels. Third week of December: brief your designer with offer details and photos. Late December: approve designs and print posters. First week of January: share the announcement and offer. Pongal week: reminders and greetings.

This keeps Pongal work out of the busiest days of the year-end rush.

Get ahead of the busiest month

December and January are packed for every local business. Planning Pongal creatives in December, alongside your year-end posts, means your festival campaign is ready before customers start shopping, and you are not designing posters on New Year's Day.

Plan Pongal before Christmas, post it before Bhogi.

Keep this year's Pongal designs and results. Next year, start from what worked and refresh it.

Pongal campaign checklist

  1. Offer. Specific, with an end date, decided in mid December.
  2. Theme. Authentic visuals, your own photos, correct Tamil.
  3. Creatives. Announcement, offer, stories, reminders, greetings.
  4. Print. Counter poster or standee booked early.
  5. Channels. Instagram, WhatsApp, Google, in-store.
  6. Timing. Live in the first week of January.

If you already have a monthly social media plan, add Pongal to the December planning session so it is designed alongside year-end posts.

For businesses that sell gifts or hampers, Pongal also brings corporate orders: companies buying sweets, dry fruits or household items for staff and clients. A simple Pongal gifting catalogue or poster aimed at businesses, shared in December, can bring in large orders before the festival rush, when corporate buyers are still finalising their plans.

Video works especially well for Pongal. A short reel of pongal boiling over in a decorated pot, your shop being prepared with kolam or your team greeting customers in traditional clothes feels warm and personal. Plan these clips in December too, so filming does not have to happen in the busiest week of the festival.

Finally, remember Kaanum Pongal and the days after. Many families visit relatives and go out, which suits restaurants, attractions and entertainment businesses. A simple post timed for those days can extend your festival campaign beyond Thai Pongal itself.

Quick answers

When should I post Pongal offers?

Share offers from the first week of January, with designs ready by late December. Reminders and greetings follow during Pongal week.

What should a Pongal social media post include?

A warm greeting, ideally in Tamil, authentic festival visuals, your brand elements and, for selling posts, a clear offer with an end date.

How early should I book Pongal printing?

Book in December. Printers are busy with year-end jobs, and early January can be crowded with festival orders.
