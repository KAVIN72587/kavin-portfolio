Edit it yourself or hire an editor?

Phone editing apps have become genuinely capable. Auto-captions, templates, music libraries and one-tap effects mean a business owner can make a decent reel in half an hour. So why would anyone pay an editor? Sometimes they should not. Sometimes it is the best money they spend.

As an editor, I have an obvious interest here, so this tries to be honest about both sides: when DIY editing makes sense, when hiring does, and a mixed approach that works for many small businesses.

01

When DIY editing makes sense

Quick, informal content: stories, simple reels of a new product, a behind-the-scenes clip, a festival greeting. Content where speed matters more than polish, such as same-day updates. When you enjoy it and have the time. When the budget is genuinely tight and you are testing whether video works for your business at all.

For these, a phone app with good captions and clean cuts is enough.

02

The hidden time cost

The question is not whether you can edit, but whether it is the best use of your time. An owner who spends three evenings a week editing reels is not spending them on customers, staff or sales. Editing also takes longer for beginners: what an experienced editor does in an hour can take an evening. Put a value on your time and compare.

03

When hiring makes sense

Videos that represent your brand for a long time: website videos, corporate films, explainers, testimonials, ads. Videos with a lot of footage to sort through, such as events, weddings, interviews and podcasts. Anything needing careful audio work, colour matching across cameras or motion graphics. And regular content at volume, where consistency matters more than any single video.

04

What an editor adds

Beyond technical skill: choosing the strongest moments from lots of footage, structuring a story, pacing that holds attention, clean audio, consistent colour, accurate captions, correctly licensed music and exports set up for each platform. An outside eye also catches what you cannot see in your own footage: the slow opening, the repeated point, the better take you skipped.

05

Templates: fast but familiar

App templates make DIY editing quick, but they are used by millions. Your reels can end up looking identical to everyone else's. An editor can build a simple, consistent style for your brand, with fonts, colours, caption style and intro and outro, which makes your content recognisable.

06

Music and rights

DIY apps often include music that is licensed for use within that app or platform, not elsewhere. A reel edited in one app with its music may get muted when posted to YouTube or used as an ad. Check the licence of any music you use, or use royalty-free tracks that cover your intended use.

07

The mixed approach

Many small businesses do both. Quick, everyday content is made in-house on a phone. Important videos, regular batches and anything with lots of footage go to an editor. Some ask an editor to create a set of branded templates, caption styles and intros once, which the team then uses for DIY content. It keeps everything consistent without paying for every clip.

08

Learning from your editor

If you plan to do some editing yourself, ask your editor to explain their choices on a few projects: why they opened with a particular shot, why a section was cut, how captions are timed. Most are happy to share. A short walkthrough of a finished edit teaches more about pacing and structure than hours of online tutorials, and your own DIY reels improve noticeably as a result.

Match the effort to the video's lifespan

A story that disappears in 24 hours does not need a professional edit. A video that sits on your homepage for two years, plays at your stall at every exhibition, or runs as an ad to thousands of people probably does.

DIY the quick stuff. Hire for what lasts or what sells.

Whichever you choose, the same basics apply: a strong opening, clear audio, captions and a clear call to action.

Quick decision guide

  1. Stories and quick updates. DIY.
  2. Weekly reels, if you enjoy it and have time. DIY, with a branded template.
  3. Monthly batch of reels. Hire, to save time and stay consistent.
  4. Website, corporate, testimonial, ads. Hire.
  5. Weddings, events, podcasts. Hire.

If you are unsure, try both for a month and compare the time spent and the results. Your own numbers will tell you more than any general advice.

Quick answers

Should I edit my business videos myself?

For quick, informal content, DIY editing with a phone app can be enough. For brand videos, ads, large amounts of footage or regular volume, a professional editor usually saves time and improves results.

Is CapCut good enough for business reels?

For simple reels it can be. Check the licensing of any music or templates, especially for ads or posting outside the app's platform.

What does a video editor do that apps cannot?

Choose the best moments from lots of footage, structure a story, pace it well, clean audio, match colour, ensure accurate captions and licensed music, and keep a consistent style.
