The school annual day video parents want

Every parent wants to see their child's moment on stage. The school wants a film that shows its culture and talent. Those are two different videos, and trying to make one serve both usually leaves everyone a little disappointed.

Here is how to plan annual day, sports day and cultural event videos: what to deliver, how to film a stage properly, how to handle audio, and how to share videos of children responsibly.

01

Two deliverables, two purposes

The full programme recording covers every performance in order, so each family can find their child. It is long and mainly for parents. The highlight film, around two to four minutes, captures the energy of the day, the best moments from many performances, the audience and the preparation. It is for the school's website, social media and next year's admissions.

Plan both from the start. They need different shots.

02

Filming the stage

For the full recording, one camera should stay wide on the stage throughout, capturing every performance completely. A second camera, closer to the stage, captures faces, expressions and details. Stage lighting is often bright on performers and dark elsewhere, so cameras need manual exposure set for the stage, not automatic settings that keep changing.

03

Audio is the hardest part

Music and speeches through a hall's sound system sound boomy and distant when recorded from the back. The best solution is a direct recording from the sound mixer, which captures clean music and microphones. Add a separate recording near the stage for applause and atmosphere. The editor combines them.

Ask the sound team in advance for a recording output from the mixer. It is the single biggest improvement.

04

Shots for the highlight film

Preparation backstage, children in costumes, teachers adjusting things, the audience reacting, parents filming on their phones, close-ups of hands on instruments, the finale, the trophy moments in sports day and the principal's key line. These make the highlight film feel like the day, not just a sequence of performances.

Short interviews also work well: a teacher on what the theme meant, a few children (with consent) saying what they enjoyed, the principal on the year. Ten seconds each, filmed in a quiet corner with a lapel mic, give the highlight film a voice beyond the stage music.

05

Consent and safeguarding

Schools have a responsibility for how images of children are used. Follow your school's photography and media policy and parental consent records. Avoid captions that name children alongside identifying details. Be careful about posting full programme recordings publicly; many schools share them privately with parents instead.

06

Sharing with parents

The full programme is best shared through a private link, an unlisted video, a password-protected page or the school's parent app, rather than public social media. Chapter markers or a list of timestamps for each performance let parents jump to their child's item. The highlight film, with consent, is the one for public channels.

07

Timing matters

Parents want the video soon after the event, while the excitement lasts. A short highlight reel within a few days, followed by the full programme within a couple of weeks, keeps everyone happy. Plan the editing schedule before the event so it does not drift into the next term.

A short teaser of thirty seconds, posted the next day, is a nice addition. It thanks parents and staff publicly, builds anticipation for the full film and gives the school something to share while the event is still being talked about in parent groups.

Every child, every item

The most common complaint about school event videos is that a child's performance was missed or cut short. For the full programme recording, the wide camera must never stop or cut away during a performance. Completeness matters more than cinematic shots.

The full recording is for every family. The highlight is for everyone else.

Give the videographer the programme order in advance, with item names and class details, so the edit can include clear titles for each performance.

Annual day video checklist

  1. Programme order. Shared with the videographer and editor in advance.
  2. Two cameras. One wide and continuous, one for close-ups.
  3. Mixer recording. Requested from the sound team.
  4. Consent. Checked against the school's media policy.
  5. Delivery plan. Highlight first, full programme with timestamps after.

With these in place, editing becomes straightforward, and parents receive a video they will actually watch and keep.

Quick answers

How long should a school annual day highlight video be?

Around two to four minutes. It captures the energy of the day through the best moments, while the full programme recording covers every performance.

How do you get good audio at a school event?

Record directly from the sound system's mixer, and add a separate recording near the stage for applause and atmosphere. The editor combines them.

Should school event videos be posted publicly?

Follow your school's media policy and parental consent. Many schools share full programme recordings privately with parents and post only consented highlight films publicly.
