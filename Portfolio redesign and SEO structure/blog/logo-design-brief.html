The logo brief that gets better concepts

The most common logo brief I receive is one line on WhatsApp: "Need a logo for my business, something modern." Every designer can work with that, but the first concepts become a guessing game. A short, thoughtful brief usually saves a whole revision round and gets you a logo that fits the business, not just the designer's taste.

You do not need design vocabulary to write a good brief. You just need honest answers to ten questions. Here they are, with a note on why each one matters.

01

1. What does your business do, in one sentence?

Not your full story, just the core: "We make home-style South Indian sweets for gifting." This keeps the designer focused on what the logo actually represents.

02

2. Who are your customers?

Age, location, income level and what they care about. A logo for a premium wedding caterer and a logo for a budget tiffin service should feel very different, even if both sell food.

03

3. Who are your main competitors?

Share two or three names or links. The designer will make sure your logo stands apart from theirs instead of accidentally looking similar.

04

4. Where will the logo appear most?

Signboard, packaging, Instagram profile, uniforms, vehicle, website? A logo used mostly as a small profile picture needs a strong simple icon. One painted on a long shop board can be wider. This question shapes the design more than any colour preference.

05

5. What should people feel when they see it?

Pick three words: trustworthy, playful, premium, traditional, modern, warm, bold. These are more useful than "I want it to look nice", because they can be compared against each concept.

06

6. Logos you like, and why

Share a few logos you admire, from any industry, and one line on what you like about each. The why matters more than the logo itself. "I like how simple it is" is useful; a screenshot with no comment is not.

07

7. What you definitely do not want

Colours you dislike, symbols that are overused in your field, styles that feel wrong. Ruling things out early is surprisingly helpful.

08

8. Does the name need Tamil, English or both?

If customers know you by a Tamil name, or you serve both audiences, say so at the start. Designing a bilingual logo is very different from adding Tamil text afterwards.

09

9. Budget and deadline

Be honest about both. A designer can suggest the right scope for your budget, and a real deadline lets them plan. If you have a launch date or a signboard being fixed, mention it.

10

10. Who makes the final decision?

If three partners need to agree, include them from the first round. Logos that are approved by one person and then rejected by another at the end cause the longest delays.

Collect feedback from all decision makers into one message for each revision round. Scattered notes over several days count as several rounds.

Ten minutes of thinking saves a week of revisions

Writing a brief forces you to think about your own business clearly, which is useful even beyond the logo. It also gives you a fair way to judge concepts: does this match the three words, does it work where we need it, does it stand apart from competitors?

A good brief doesn't limit the designer. It points them in the right direction.

If writing feels like a chore, a 20-minute call where the designer asks these questions works just as well. Either way, the answers need to exist before the first concept.

A simple brief template

Business: one sentence Customers: who, where, what they care about Competitors: 2–3 names or links Main uses: signboard / packaging / social / uniform / web Three words: ___, ___, ___ Logos I like and why: ... Avoid: colours, symbols, styles Language: Tamil / English / both Budget and deadline: ... Decision makers: names

Copy this into a message or document, fill it in and send it with your quote request.

Do not worry if some answers are short or uncertain. "Not sure yet" is a perfectly useful answer, because it tells the designer where to offer options. What causes trouble is not missing answers but hidden ones: a colour the owner secretly hates, or a partner who was never asked. Put everything on the table at the start, and the first concepts will be far closer to what you need.

Quick answers

What should a logo design brief include?

Your business in one sentence, target customers, competitors, where the logo will be used, three words describing the feeling, examples you like and dislike, language, budget, deadline and who approves it.

Do I need to know what I want before hiring a logo designer?

No. You need to know your business and customers. A good designer turns that information into visual ideas; your job is to explain the business clearly.

How long should a logo brief be?

One page is enough. Clear answers to the key questions matter more than length.
