Your reel has three seconds

On a reel or a short, the viewer decides almost instantly whether to keep watching. If the first moment is a logo, a slow pan of the shop front or someone saying "Hi everyone, welcome back", most people are already gone.

The opening is the most valuable part of any short video, and it is usually the least planned. Here is what makes an opening work, six hook types that suit small businesses, and why the hook has to be decided before the camera rolls, not in the edit.

01

Start with the most interesting moment

The rule is simple: lead with the best thing. The finished cake, not the flour. The transformation, not the before. The surprising number, not the introduction. You can explain how you got there afterwards, once the viewer has decided to stay.

In the edit, this often means moving a moment from the middle or end of the footage to the very front, then cutting back to the start.

02

Words on screen in the first frame

Most people watch with the sound off at first. A short line of text on screen from the very first frame tells them what the video is about and why they should care. Keep it under eight words, large, and placed where the platform's buttons will not cover it.

Write the on-screen hook as a promise: what will the viewer know, see or get by the end?

03

Six hooks that suit small businesses

The result first: show the finished product, room or haircut, then rewind. A common mistake: "Most people get this wrong when buying…". A number: "3 things to check before…". A question your customers actually ask. A strong visual action: pouring, cutting, unboxing, a reveal. A direct callout: "If you run a clinic in Chennai, watch this."

Pick one per video. Stacking three hooks in the opening just makes it noisy.

04

Movement and sound

A static wide shot is easy to scroll past. Something moving towards the camera, a quick cut, a hand entering the frame or a strong sound effect all catch attention. The first shot should usually be a close-up, because reels are watched on small screens.

05

Then keep the promise

A hook that over-promises gets the view and loses the trust. If the opening says "3 mistakes", show three mistakes, quickly. Viewers who feel tricked leave and do not come back. A good hook is an honest summary of the best part of the video, delivered first.

06

Test hooks, not whole videos

If you post regularly, the cheapest experiment is to change only the opening. Use the same footage and the same message with two different first shots or two different on-screen lines, posted a week or two apart. Instagram and YouTube both show how many viewers stayed past the first few seconds, and that number tells you more than likes do.

Over a few months, you will see which openings your audience responds to: perhaps questions beat numbers, or the result-first shot always wins. Ask your editor to cut two alternative openings when you order a batch of reels. It takes a few extra minutes and gives you real data instead of guesses.

The hook is planned at the shoot

Editors can rearrange footage, add text and choose the strongest opening shot, but only from what was filmed. If nobody shot a close-up of the finished result, or a clean line to camera stating the point, there is nothing strong to open with.

Decide the first shot before you press record.

When you plan a reel, write the hook first: the opening shot and the on-screen line. Then shoot that deliberately, ideally two or three ways, so the editor has options.

Hook check before you post

  1. Watch the first two seconds only. Do you know what the video is about?
  2. Mute it. Does the on-screen text still carry the hook?
  3. Check the safe area. Is anything hidden under the caption or buttons?
  4. No slow intros. No logo animation or greeting before the point.
  5. Check the cover. The thumbnail on your grid should match the hook, not a random frame.

If the opening fails any of these, fix it before posting. Re-uploading a reel later rarely recovers the reach the first upload lost.

Quick answers

How long do I have to hook viewers on a reel?

Roughly the first one to three seconds. Lead with the most interesting moment and a short on-screen line saying what the video is about.

Should a business reel start with a logo?

Usually not. A logo intro gives viewers a reason to scroll. Put branding subtly in a corner or at the end, and open with the most interesting moment.

Can an editor add a hook to an existing video?

Often yes, by moving the strongest moment to the start and adding on-screen text. It works best when the shoot captured a clear close-up or line that can be used.
