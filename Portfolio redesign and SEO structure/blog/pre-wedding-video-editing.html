A pre-wedding video that tells your story

Many pre-wedding videos look beautiful and say very little: three minutes of slow-motion walks, flowing dupattas and drone shots set to a popular song. The ones people remember feel personal. They show something about the couple, not just a location and a costume change.

Here is how to plan a pre-wedding video that feels like yours: the right length, choosing music that will not get muted, building a simple story, and the versions you will need for Instagram, WhatsApp and the wedding screen.

01

Decide where it will be shown

A pre-wedding video is usually shown in three places: Instagram and WhatsApp before the wedding, the LED screen at the reception, and your own collection. Each needs a slightly different version. A 30 to 60 second vertical reel for social media, a two to four minute film for the screen and the family, and perhaps a short teaser. Plan this before the shoot, so the videographer films for both vertical and horizontal.

02

Two to four minutes is plenty

Longer pre-wedding films rarely hold attention, even among relatives. Two to four minutes for the main film, built around one song or a short piece of music, is enough to feel complete. At the reception, guests are talking and moving around, so a shorter, punchier version often works better on the screen than a long one.

03

A story, not a slideshow

Even a simple story makes a big difference. How you met, a shared hobby, a place that matters to you both, a small inside joke, the proposal. A voice-over of each of you talking about the other, recorded separately and honestly, can turn a pretty video into a moving one. The visuals then illustrate the story rather than just showing locations.

A few minutes of natural audio, talking, laughing, even a small argument about directions, adds more personality than any slow-motion shot.

04

Music: choose carefully

Popular film songs are the most common choice and the most common problem. On Instagram, YouTube and some other platforms, copyrighted songs can lead to muted audio, blocked videos or claims. At a private reception screen it may not be an issue, but online it often is. Options include licensed music, instrumental tracks from royalty-free libraries, or adding the popular song inside the Instagram app where its library allows.

05

Locations and outfits

Fewer locations, used well, beat many locations rushed. Two or three places with meaning, at the right time of day, give the editor more good material than six places with an hour at each. The same applies to outfits: two or three changes is plenty, and each change takes time away from filming.

06

What makes a strong edit

Pacing that follows the music, with cuts on the beat at energetic moments and longer shots in quiet ones. Genuine moments, laughs, glances and mistakes, rather than only posed shots. Consistent colour across locations. A clear opening and ending. And restraint: not every beautiful shot needs to be in the film.

07

Give your editor what they need

Tell the editor the purpose, the versions you need, the music or the mood, any story or voice-over, and the moments you love. If you have a reference video, share it and say what you like about it. Mention names and dates for the title card, spelled correctly.

08

Plan the timeline backwards

Work back from the date you want to share it. If the reel should go up a month before the wedding, the shoot needs to happen six to eight weeks before, leaving time for editing, one round of changes and any music decisions. Shooting the week before the wedding leaves no time for revisions, and the edit competes with every other wedding job on the same calendar.

Make it about the two of you

The locations, costumes and drone shots will look like many other pre-wedding videos. What will not look like anyone else's is your story, your voices and your real moments. Those are what family members will remember, and what you will want to watch in twenty years.

Film fewer places and more of yourselves.

If you are nervous on camera, plan activities rather than poses: cooking together, a walk with a chai, a game. Natural moments happen when you are doing something.

Versions to ask for

  1. Main film. Two to four minutes, horizontal, for the screen and family.
  2. Reel. 30 to 60 seconds, vertical, for Instagram and WhatsApp status.
  3. Teaser. 15 seconds, to announce the date.
  4. Screen version. Checked for the LED wall's shape and brightness if possible.

Ask for the reel to use music that is safe for social media, even if the main film uses a song only for the private screening.

Quick answers

How long should a pre-wedding video be?

Two to four minutes for the main film, plus a 30 to 60 second vertical reel for social media and possibly a short teaser.

Can I use a Bollywood or Tamil film song in my pre-wedding video?

For private screenings it may not be an issue, but online platforms may mute or block videos with copyrighted songs. Use licensed or royalty-free music for versions you post publicly.

How can we look natural in a pre-wedding shoot?

Plan activities rather than poses, choose meaningful locations, and record natural conversation. Real moments make the best footage.
