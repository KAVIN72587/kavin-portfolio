The brief that gets you the wedding film you want

Your editor was not at your wedding, or saw it through a viewfinder while working. They do not know that your grandmother travelled from Madurai, that the cousin in the blue shirt organised everything, or that the moment you care most about happened during the reception when nobody was watching. Unless you tell them.

A short, clear brief before editing starts is the most effective way to get a wedding film that feels like yours, and to avoid long revision rounds afterwards. Here is what to include.

01

The people who must appear

List the people who must be in the film, with a way to identify them: "grandmother in the green saree", "my brother, best man, tall with glasses". Parents, grandparents, siblings, close friends, anyone who has passed away since and whose presence matters. Editors work from hours of footage with hundreds of faces; this list makes sure nobody important is missed.

02

Rituals and moments that matter

Which rituals or moments are most important to you and your families? The muhurtham, thali tying, saptapadi, kanyadaanam, a particular speech, the baraat, an entry, a dance. Say which ones should feature in the highlight film and which should be complete in the full film. If a moment happened off-schedule, describe when and where.

If you know roughly when a moment happened, give the time. It saves the editor searching hours of footage.

03

Music

Share songs you love, songs that matter to you as a couple, and songs you never want to hear. For films that will be posted online, discuss with the editor which music is safe to use publicly and which only for private viewing. If you have a song that was played at the wedding, mention it; it can be used naturally from the venue audio.

04

Length and style

Agree the lengths of each deliverable: a teaser, a highlight film, the full film. Share one or two reference films you like and say specifically what you like: the pace, the colour, the use of speeches, the emotion. "Cinematic" means different things to different people; a reference link removes the guesswork.

05

Names, spellings and dates

Write down your names exactly as you want them on screen, the date, the venue name and any hashtags. Tamil, Telugu or Hindi spellings in English vary, so be precise. If you want a dedication or a line of text at the start or end, write it out.

06

What to leave out

Just as important: moments, people or shots you do not want. An unflattering angle you dislike, a relative who should not feature prominently, a moment that went wrong. Editors are discreet, and telling them in advance is far easier than asking for removals later.

07

One brief from both families

Agree the brief between the couple first, then share it as one document. Weddings involve two families, each with their own priorities. A single agreed brief avoids contradictory feedback later, and gives the editor clear direction from the start.

08

Agree how feedback will work

Decide now who will watch the first draft and how notes will be sent. The simplest approach is for the couple to watch together, then send one list of timestamped notes. Feedback trickling in over weeks from parents, siblings and aunts, often contradicting each other, is the most common reason wedding films are delayed. Ask the editor how many revision rounds are included before you begin.

Ten minutes of writing saves weeks

Most wedding film revisions come from things the editor could not have known: a missing grandparent, a song the couple dislikes, a ritual that mattered more than it seemed. A brief written before editing begins prevents most of them.

The editor knows how to make a film. Only you know what your wedding meant.

Send the brief together with the footage handover, or as soon as possible after the wedding, while the details are fresh.

Wedding edit brief template

Couple names (on screen): Date / venue: Must-include people (and how to recognise them): Most important rituals and moments (with rough times): Songs we love / songs to avoid: Lengths: teaser __ highlight __ full film __ Reference films (and what we like): Anything to leave out: One contact for feedback:

Fill it in together, send it once, and keep a copy. It becomes the checklist you use when reviewing the first draft.

Quick answers

What should I tell my wedding videographer or editor?

The people who must appear, the most important rituals and moments, music preferences, desired lengths, style references, correct spellings of names and anything you want left out.

Can I choose the songs for my wedding video?

Usually yes. Share songs you love and dislike, and discuss with the editor which music can be used safely if the film will be posted online.

How do I avoid endless revisions on my wedding film?

Send a clear brief before editing starts, agree it between both families, and send one consolidated, timestamped list of feedback per round.
