The button that turns visits into enquiries

Many small business websites are pleasant to browse and hard to act on. Visitors read about the services, like what they see, and then cannot quickly find what to do next. Or they find five different options and choose none. The call to action is the small part of the page that decides whether a visit becomes an enquiry.

Here is how to decide on the main action, word buttons so people want to press them, place them where visitors look, and make sure something good happens after the click.

01

Decide the one main action

Every page should have one primary action you want visitors to take. For most local businesses, it is "WhatsApp us", "Call now", "Book a visit" or "Get a quote". Secondary options can exist, but they should look secondary. When every button is equally loud, visitors hesitate.

Choose the action that matches how your customers prefer to contact you. If most enquiries come by WhatsApp, make that the main button.

02

Word it as the visitor's benefit

"Submit", "Click here" and "Contact us" are vague. Better buttons say what happens: "Get a quote in 24 hours", "Book a free trial class", "Check availability on WhatsApp", "See the full menu". Start with a verb, be specific, and keep it short enough to fit on one line on a phone.

03

Reduce the fear

A short line beside or below the button can remove hesitation: "No obligation", "Free first consultation", "We reply within two hours", "Prices from ₹…". People hesitate because they are unsure what happens next or worry about being pressured. Answer that worry in a few words.

04

Place it where eyes go

On mobile, the main action should be visible on the first screen without scrolling, and again after each major section: after services, after testimonials, at the end. A sticky button at the bottom of the screen on mobile, such as a WhatsApp or call bar, works well for many local businesses. Do not make visitors scroll back to the top to act.

Test on your phone: from anywhere on the page, can you contact the business in one tap?

05

Make it look like a button

Buttons should look tappable: a solid colour that contrasts with the background, enough padding, and a size comfortable for a thumb. Text links in a paragraph are easy to miss. Keep the main button colour consistent across the site so visitors learn what it looks like.

06

Make the next step easy

If the button opens a form, keep it short: name, phone and one question about what they need is often enough. If it opens WhatsApp, prefill a helpful message. If it is a phone number, make sure it is tappable and answered. Every extra step after the click loses some people.

07

Follow through fast

The best call to action is wasted if the enquiry sits unanswered. Reply quickly, ideally within the hour during business hours. Show a clear confirmation after a form is submitted, with what happens next and when. Test the whole flow monthly; broken forms are surprisingly common.

08

Measure which buttons work

Set up click tracking on the main buttons in Google Analytics, so you can see how many people tap WhatsApp, call or submit the form, and from which pages. If a page gets plenty of visitors but almost no clicks, the button may be hidden, the wording unclear or the offer unconvincing. Change one thing at a time and compare the next month's numbers.

One page, one clear next step

A visitor who has read your services page is interested. The job of the call to action is to make the next step obvious, easy and low-risk. Clarity beats cleverness every time.

Tell visitors exactly what to do, and what happens when they do it.

Look at your home page on a phone. Within three seconds, is it clear what you want visitors to do? If not, that is the first thing to fix.

Button wording that works

  1. Instead of "Submit". "Send my enquiry" or "Get my quote".
  2. Instead of "Contact us". "WhatsApp us now" or "Book a call".
  3. Instead of "Learn more". "See prices" or "See our work".
  4. Instead of "Click here". Describe what is behind the click.

Small wording changes on the main button are one of the cheapest website improvements you can make, and one of the easiest to test.

Quick answers

What is a call to action on a website?

It is the prompt, usually a button, that tells visitors what to do next, such as WhatsApp us, book a visit or get a quote.

Where should the call to action be placed?

On the first screen without scrolling, especially on mobile, and again after key sections. A sticky WhatsApp or call bar on mobile works well for local businesses.

How many calls to action should a page have?

One primary action per page, repeated in several places. Secondary options can exist but should look less prominent.
