Your domain expired. Now what?

The website stops loading. Emails to your business address bounce. Someone checks and finds the domain expired last week. It is one of the most common website emergencies for small businesses, and usually one of the most avoidable.

Here is what happens after a domain expires, how long you typically have to recover it, and the settings that make sure it never happens again. Exact timings and fees vary by registrar and by domain ending, so check with yours, but the stages are broadly the same.

01

Stage 1: the grace period

Right after expiry, most registrars give a grace period, often around 30 days for .com and similar for many other endings. The site and email usually stop working, but you can renew at the normal price, and everything comes back once the renewal goes through and settings refresh.

If you are in this stage, renew immediately. Log into the registrar account, renew for more than one year if you can, and check the site and email within a few hours.

02

Stage 2: redemption

After the grace period, many domains enter a redemption period. The domain can still be recovered by the original owner, but registrars charge a recovery fee on top of renewal, sometimes several thousand rupees. It is painful, but far cheaper than losing the name.

Contact the registrar's support directly at this stage. The process is not always available as a button in the dashboard.

03

Stage 3: release and auction

If nobody redeems it, the domain is released. Popular or older names are often picked up quickly by automated systems or listed at auction, sometimes by people who want to sell it back to you at a high price, or to use the traffic for something else entirely.

An established business domain has search rankings and backlinks. That makes it attractive to others once it lapses.

04

Why it happens

The usual reasons: the renewal card expired, the reminder emails went to an old address or a designer's inbox, or nobody knew who had bought the domain in the first place. Sometimes the domain is registered in a former employee's or designer's account, and they never saw the warnings or have since moved on.

05

If the domain is in someone else's account

If a designer or agency registered the domain under their own account, you may not be able to renew it yourself. Ask them to renew it now, and then to transfer it to an account in your name. Domain registrars have a standard transfer process using an authorisation code. Do this while relations are good, not during an emergency.

06

Recover email and site after renewal

Once renewed, the website and email usually come back within a few hours as settings refresh around the internet. If they do not, check that the domain's name servers and DNS records were not reset during expiry. Some registrars park expired domains on their own servers, and the original settings need restoring. Keep a screenshot of your DNS records for exactly this reason.

Make expiry impossible

Three settings remove almost all risk. Turn on auto-renew. Keep a valid card on the account and update it when the old one expires. Make sure the account email is one you read every day, not an old Gmail or your designer's address.

Renew for five years, turn on auto-renew, and use an email you actually read.

Renewing for several years at once costs a little more upfront and removes the yearly risk. For a business that depends on its website and email, it is one of the cheapest forms of insurance available.

Domain safety checklist

  1. Owner name. The registrant is your business, not a designer or ex-employee.
  2. Account email. An address you read daily, ideally not on the same domain.
  3. Auto-renew on. With a card that will still be valid at renewal time.
  4. Expiry date noted. In your calendar, with a reminder a month before.
  5. DNS screenshot. So settings can be restored quickly if anything resets.

Go through this list once a year, ideally in the same month as your domain renews. It takes ten minutes and covers the only failure that can take your website and your email down at the same moment.

Quick answers

What happens when a domain expires?

The website and email usually stop working. Most registrars give a grace period to renew at normal price, then a redemption period with a recovery fee, after which the domain can be released for anyone to register.

Can I get my expired domain back?

Usually yes if you act during the grace or redemption period. Contact your registrar immediately. Once the domain is released and registered by someone else, recovery is much harder.

Why should the account email not be on the same domain?

Because if the domain expires, email on that domain stops working too, and you will not receive the registrar's urgent warnings. Use a separate address you check daily.
