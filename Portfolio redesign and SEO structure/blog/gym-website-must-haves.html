A gym website that gets people through the door

Someone decides they finally want to start working out. They search for gyms nearby, open three websites, and choose the one where they can quickly see the plans, the timings and what it looks like inside. The one that says "call for details" usually loses.

Here is what a gym, yoga studio, dance class or fitness centre website needs, how to present plans and classes, and why a free trial is often the most important button on the page.

01

Make the first visit easy

The biggest barrier for new members is walking in for the first time. Offer a free or low-cost trial session and make it the main button: "Book a free trial." Link it to WhatsApp or a short form asking for name, phone and preferred time. Say what will happen at the trial so nervous beginners know what to expect.

Respond quickly. A trial request answered the next day is often a lead that already joined somewhere else.

02

Show plans and prices

Monthly, quarterly and yearly plans with prices, what each includes, and any joining fee. Prospective members compare gyms on this, and a site that hides it sends them elsewhere. If prices vary or change with offers, show the regular price and mention that current offers are available on enquiry.

Keep the plans to three or four. Long lists of variations confuse people at the exact moment you want them to decide.

03

Class timetable and timings

Opening hours, peak and quiet times, and a timetable for classes such as yoga, Zumba or strength training. Mark separate timings for women's batches if you have them. A timetable as a proper web page or table, not an image, reads better on phones and can be updated easily.

If your timetable changes often, choose a setup where you can edit it in a few minutes.

04

Trainers and their qualifications

Introduce the trainers with photos, certifications and what they specialise in, such as weight loss, strength, seniors or women's fitness. Members join gyms; they stay because of trainers. A personal introduction from the trainer, even a short video, helps beginners feel comfortable.

If you offer personal training, explain how it works: how sessions are booked, how long they last, and roughly what it costs on top of membership. It is often the most profitable service a gym sells and the least explained on its website.

05

Real photos of the space

Real photos of the equipment, the floor, the changing rooms and the classes in progress. Stock photos of models in a gym that is clearly not yours put people off when they visit and see the real thing. Photograph the space in good light, tidy, at a quiet time.

A short walkthrough video helps too. Thirty seconds of walking from the entrance through the floor, the cardio area, the weights section and the changing rooms answers dozens of unasked questions, and reassures beginners who are nervous about walking into a gym for the first time. It also gives you a ready-made reel for Instagram.

06

Be careful with results and claims

Before-and-after transformations can be powerful, but only with the member's written consent, and only if they are genuine and typical enough not to mislead. Avoid promises like "lose 10 kg in 30 days", which are often misleading, and medical claims about curing conditions. Describe the programmes, the support and the experience instead.

Location decides most memberships

Most people join a gym within a short distance of home or work. Your website should make the location obvious: area name in the headline, a map, landmarks, parking. It also helps you appear in searches like "gym near me" together with your Google Business Profile.

Area name in the headline, map on the page, trial button everywhere.

If you have more than one branch, give each branch its own page with its own timings, address and photos.

The fitness website checklist

  1. Trial button. Free or low-cost first session, linked to WhatsApp or a form.
  2. Plans. Three or four, with prices and what is included.
  3. Timetable. As a web page, easy to update.
  4. Trainers. Photos, certifications, specialities.
  5. Location. Area, map, parking, hours.

A three-page or even one-page website covers all of this. What matters more than page count is that the trial button works, the prices and timetable are current, and someone answers trial requests the same day.

Quick answers

What should a gym website include?

Membership plans and prices, class timetable and opening hours, trainer profiles, real photos of the facility, a free trial booking option and clear location details.

Should a gym show membership prices online?

Yes, at least the regular plan prices. People compare gyms on price, and a site that hides it often loses the enquiry to one that shows it.

Can a gym use before-and-after photos on its website?

Only with the member's written consent, and only if they are genuine and not misleading. Avoid promises of specific weight loss in a set time and any medical claims.
