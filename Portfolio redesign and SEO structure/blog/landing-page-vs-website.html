Landing page or website: where should your ads go?

You are about to spend ₹10,000 on Instagram or Google ads. The question nobody asks until the money is gone: when someone taps the ad, what exactly do they land on?

Most small businesses send ad traffic to their home page, because that is what they have. The home page then does what home pages do: introduces the business, lists every service, links to the about page, the gallery and the blog. The visitor who tapped an ad for one specific offer has to go looking for it, and most do not.

A landing page and a website are not competitors. They do different jobs. Here is how to tell which job you need done.

01

A website is a shop; a landing page is a counter

A website answers every question someone might have about your business: what you do, where you are, who you are, what others say about you. It is built for people who arrive from many directions — Google, your visiting card, a friend's recommendation — with many different questions.

A landing page answers one question for one kind of visitor and asks them to do one thing: book, call, enquire, buy. It usually has no menu, because a menu is a door out.

Simple test: if the page has one button that matters, it is a landing page. If it has a menu, it is a website.

02

Paid ads usually belong on a landing page

When you pay for each click, every visitor who wanders off is money spent for nothing. A landing page built for the ad — same offer, same words, same photo — feels like a continuation of the ad rather than a fresh start. That consistency is what makes people stay long enough to act.

It also makes the ad easier to measure. One page, one button, one number to watch: how many of the people who arrived pressed it.

03

WhatsApp and Instagram campaigns can skip the page entirely

If your goal is a conversation, not a form, a click-to-WhatsApp ad or a link in bio that opens a chat may beat any page. A landing page earns its place when people need convincing first — prices, photos, reviews, a short video — before they are ready to message you.

04

Google needs a website

Landing pages are poor at being found in search. They are short, focused on one offer, and often hidden from Google on purpose so they do not compete with your main pages. If you want to appear when someone searches "dentist in Velachery" or "catering in Anna Nagar", you need a proper website with a page for each thing you offer.

Your Google Business Profile should link to your website, not to an ad landing page.

05

Most businesses end up with both

The usual order is: a small website first so you exist and can be found, then a landing page each time you run a campaign worth spending money on. Seasonal offers, a new branch opening, admissions season, a product launch — each gets its own page, and the page can be retired when the campaign ends.

The page has to match the ad

The most common landing page mistake is not design. It is mismatch. The ad says "Free consultation this week" and the page says "Welcome to our clinic". The visitor has to work out whether they are in the right place, and many do not bother.

The headline on the page should repeat the promise in the ad, almost word for word.

Same offer, same photo, same price, same words. It feels repetitive to you because you wrote both. To the visitor, it feels like the right door.

What a landing page needs, top to bottom

Keep it to what the visitor needs to decide. Everything else is a distraction.

  1. A headline that repeats the ad's promise. One sentence, in the visitor's words, not your company's.
  2. Proof, near the top. Real photos, a rating, the names of clients you have served, or the number of years you have been doing this.
  3. The details that decide it. Price or price range, location, timings, what happens after they enquire.
  4. One clear button, repeated. Call, WhatsApp, or a short form — pick one and put it near the top and again at the end.
  5. Fast loading on a phone. Most ad clicks in India come from mobile data. A page that takes six seconds loses people before they see it.

Quick answers

Can my landing page be part of my website?

Yes, and that is usually best. It lives on your own domain, for example yourbusiness.com/offer, shares your branding, and costs nothing extra to host. It simply has no menu and is not linked from the main navigation.

Is a one-page website the same as a landing page?

Not quite. A one-page website introduces the whole business on a single scrolling page. A landing page is built around one offer for one campaign. A well-made one-page site can work as a landing page for simple businesses with one main service.

How much does a landing page cost?

It depends on the content and whether it needs forms, tracking or payments. My single-page sites start at ₹9,000 and are live within a week; a campaign page on an existing site I built is usually less, because the design system already exists.
