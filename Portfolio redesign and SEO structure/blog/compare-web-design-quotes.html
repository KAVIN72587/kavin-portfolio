Comparing web design quotes

Ask five designers for a website quote and you may get five very different numbers, from a few thousand rupees to over a lakh. It is tempting to pick the cheapest or assume the most expensive is the best. Neither works. The quotes are usually for different things.

Here is how to compare web design quotes so you are comparing like with like: what to check in each one, the questions to ask, and the hidden costs that turn a cheap quote into an expensive website.

01

Send everyone the same brief

Quotes vary partly because each designer imagines a different project. A written brief with your goals, pages, features, content status, budget and deadline makes every quote answer the same question. Without it, you are comparing guesses.

Give the same information to everyone, including your budget range and deadline. Designers who receive a clear brief can also tell you quickly if the project is not a good fit for them, which saves you a wasted call. Notice who asks sensible follow-up questions; it is often the best sign of how they will handle the project.

02

Check the scope line by line

Number of pages, design approach (template or custom), mobile design, contact forms, WhatsApp integration, basic SEO setup, analytics, speed optimisation, number of revision rounds, and training. A quote that lists these clearly is easier to compare and less likely to surprise you.

03

Who writes the content?

Some quotes include writing or editing your text; most assume you supply it. Photography may or may not be included. Content is the biggest hidden cost and the biggest cause of delays, so ask directly: what do I need to provide, and what happens if I cannot?

04

Ownership and accounts

Ask who registers the domain and hosting, and in whose name. Ask whether you get full admin access at handover. Some low-cost offers keep the site on the provider's account or platform, which makes leaving difficult. Insist that everything is in your name, with logins handed over.

A cheap site you do not own can become the most expensive one when you try to move it.

05

Running costs and support

Ask what you will pay each year after launch: domain, hosting, email, plugins, maintenance. Ask what support is included after launch, for how long, and what changes cost afterwards. A quote that is cheap to build but has high mandatory monthly fees may cost more over two or three years.

06

Timeline and process

Compare the proposed schedule and process. A clear plan with stages, review points and a launch date shows organisation. Vague promises like "ready soon" are a warning sign. Ask how many projects they handle at once.

07

Look at their actual work

Ask for live websites they have built recently, similar in size to yours. Open them on your phone. Are they fast? Easy to use? Do the forms work? Ask if you can speak to one or two past clients. Recent, live, working sites tell you more than a polished portfolio page.

08

Payment terms

Compare how and when you pay. A common, fair arrangement is an advance to book the slot and the balance on completion, sometimes with a middle milestone for larger projects. Be wary of quotes asking for full payment upfront, and of agreements that do not say what happens if the project is delayed or cancelled. Clear payment stages tied to visible progress protect both sides.

Cheapest and most expensive can both be wrong

A very low quote often means a template with your text pasted in, no SEO setup, little testing and limited support. A very high quote may include strategy and features you do not need. The right quote is the one that matches your goals, with clear scope and ownership.

Compare what you get and what you own, not just the number.

If two quotes look similar in price but differ in scope, ask each designer to explain the difference. Their answer will tell you a lot about how they work.

Questions to ask every designer

  1. What exactly is included? Pages, features, revisions, SEO setup, training.
  2. What do I need to provide? Text, photos, logo, accounts.
  3. Whose name are the accounts in? Domain, hosting, admin access.
  4. What will I pay each year? Running costs and support.
  5. When will it launch? With a schedule and review points.

Put the answers side by side in a simple table. The differences between quotes usually become obvious.

Quick answers

Why do web design quotes vary so much?

Because they often describe different things: template vs custom design, content included or not, different features, ownership arrangements, support and running costs.

Should I choose the cheapest website quote?

Only if it includes what you need and leaves you owning the site and accounts. Check scope, ownership, running costs and support before deciding.

What should a web design quote include?

Pages and features, design approach, revision rounds, SEO and analytics setup, timeline, what you must provide, ownership of accounts, running costs and support.
