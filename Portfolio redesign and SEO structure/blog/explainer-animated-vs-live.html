Animated explainer or live action?

You need a short video that explains what your business does. One option is to film it: people, products, places. The other is to animate it: graphics, icons and text in motion. Both work. They are good at different things.

Here is what each format does well, what affects cost and time, and a simple way to choose based on what you sell and who you are selling to.

01

Animation explains the invisible

Animation is strongest when the thing you sell is hard to film: software, a financial product, a process, a service that happens behind the scenes. You can show data moving, steps simplifying, problems disappearing. Everything on screen is designed, so the message stays tightly controlled.

It also avoids the logistics of a shoot: no location, no actors, no weather, no lighting.

02

Live action builds trust in people and products

If customers buy from you because of who you are or what they can touch, film it. A clinic, a restaurant, a manufacturing unit, a salon, a handmade product. Real faces, real spaces and real products answer the question "is this genuine?" in a way that animation cannot.

Live action also tends to feel warmer, which matters for services where people are trusting you personally.

It does not need to be an expensive production. A well-lit phone shoot of your actual space, your actual team and your actual product, edited tightly with clear text on screen, often outperforms a polished but generic film, because viewers can tell it is real.

03

Cost and time

Animated explainers are built from your script, so there is no shoot cost, but design and animation take time. A 60 to 90 second explainer typically takes one to two weeks. Live action needs a shoot (a videographer, a location, sometimes people on camera) plus editing, but the edit itself can be faster.

04

Updating later

Animation is easier to update. A price change, a new feature or a new logo can usually be changed in the project without starting again. Live action is harder to update: if a product changes or a staff member in the video leaves, you may need to reshoot. For fast-changing products, that alone can decide it.

05

The script matters more than the format

Both formats fail with a weak script. Before choosing, write down in one sentence what the viewer should understand and what they should do next. Then write the script around the problem the customer has, not around your company history. A 60-second video is roughly 130 to 150 spoken words; that is shorter than most people expect.

If the script works read aloud over a blank screen, the video will work in either format.

06

Mixing both

Many good business videos mix the two: filmed footage of people and products, with animated text, diagrams or screen recordings layered in to explain the details. This is often the most practical option for small businesses, because it uses phone or simple footage for authenticity and graphics for clarity.

A quick way to decide

Ask: would a customer want to see the real thing before buying? If yes, film it. Is the thing hard or impossible to film? Animate it. Is it both? Mix them.

Film what people need to trust. Animate what people need to understand.

Then consider budget, timeline and how often the content will change. Those usually confirm the choice rather than change it.

Which format fits

  1. Software, apps, fintech. Animation or screen recording with motion graphics.
  2. Clinics, salons, restaurants. Live action, with simple text graphics.
  3. Manufacturing, B2B services. Live action of the facility, with animated diagrams.
  4. Training and onboarding. Animation for processes, live action for the people.

Whatever you choose, plan for more than one placement. A 60 to 90 second main video for the website, a 15 to 30 second vertical cut for reels and ads, and perhaps a silent version with captions for LinkedIn. Asking for these at the start costs far less than requesting them after the main video is finished.

Also ask for the final project files or at least the separate graphics, so future updates do not mean starting from scratch.

Quick answers

Is an animated explainer cheaper than live action?

Not always. Animation avoids shoot costs but takes longer to design and animate. Live action needs a shoot but can edit faster. Compare total cost, including shooting, for your specific video.

How long should an explainer video be?

Usually 60 to 90 seconds. That is about 130 to 200 spoken words, enough to explain one problem, your solution and the next step.

Can I combine animation and filmed footage?

Yes. Many effective business videos use filmed footage for people and products, with animated text, diagrams or screen recordings to explain details.
