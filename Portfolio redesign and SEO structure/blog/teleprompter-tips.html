Reading from a teleprompter, naturally

Teleprompter apps are a gift for anyone who freezes on camera or needs to get precise information right. They are also the reason many business videos sound like someone reading a notice aloud. The difference between the two is mostly in how the script is written and how the prompter is set up.

Here is how to write for a teleprompter, set it up so your eyes do not wander, rehearse just enough, and record in a way that gives your editor clean material.

01

Write the way you speak

A prompter amplifies the style of the script. Formal, written sentences sound formal and written. Write short sentences in your own everyday words, use contractions, and read every line aloud while writing. If a line makes you stumble at the desk, it will make you stumble on camera.

Write numbers the way you say them: "twenty thousand" rather than "₹20,000" if you read figures awkwardly.

02

Short lines, big text

Set the text large and the lines short, around five to eight words per line, so your eyes barely move. Wide lines of small text make your eyes sweep left and right, which viewers notice immediately. Many apps let you narrow the text width; do it.

Use a high-contrast setting, such as white text on a black background, and turn the screen brightness up. In a bright room or near a window, faint text forces you to squint, and that shows on camera. A mirrored prompter needs the text flipped; most apps have a mirror setting for this.

03

Position the prompter at the lens

The text should be as close to the camera lens as possible. A proper teleprompter uses a glass mirror in front of the lens, so you look directly into it while reading. With a phone app, put the text at the top of the screen just under the front camera, or use a second device placed directly beside or below the lens. Sit or stand far enough back that the small eye movement is not obvious.

The further you are from the camera, the less visible your reading becomes.

04

Set the speed slightly slow

Prompters scroll at a set speed, and people tend to speed up to keep up. Set it a little slower than feels natural, so you can pause and breathe. Some apps follow your voice and scroll as you speak, which helps a lot. Have someone else control the speed if possible.

05

Mark pauses and emphasis

Add a line break or a symbol like "//" where you want to pause, and capitals or bold for the one word in a sentence you want to stress. It turns flat reading into speech with rhythm. Do not overdo it: one or two marks per paragraph.

06

Rehearse twice, not ten times

Read the script aloud twice before recording: once to catch awkward lines, once at performance pace. Fix anything you stumble on. Over-rehearsing makes delivery mechanical. You want to know the script well enough that the prompter is a reminder, not a lifeline.

During the rehearsal, notice any line where you naturally say something different from what is written. Change the script to match what you said. Your spoken version is almost always more natural than the written one.

07

Record in sections

Do not try to read the whole script perfectly in one take. Record paragraph by paragraph, and if you stumble, pause and restart the sentence. Leave a second of silence before and after each section. The editor can then pick the best takes and join them smoothly, covering joins with B-roll.

Talk to one person

The most natural-sounding prompter readers imagine a specific person behind the lens: a customer they like, a friend, a new employee. They are explaining, not announcing. That small mental shift changes tone, pace and expression more than any technical setting.

You are explaining to one person, not reading to a crowd.

Smile slightly at the start of each section and let your hands move naturally. Viewers forgive an occasional glance; they do not forgive a monotone.

Prompter setup checklist

  1. Script. Short, spoken sentences, read aloud while writing.
  2. Text. Large, five to eight words per line.
  3. Position. As close to the lens as possible.
  4. Speed. Slightly slow, or voice-following.
  5. Recording. Section by section, with pauses between.

With this setup, most people sound noticeably more natural by the third or fourth section, once they forget about the device and focus on the message.

Quick answers

How do I use a teleprompter without looking like I'm reading?

Write short, conversational sentences, use large text with narrow lines, place the text close to the lens, sit further back, keep the speed slightly slow and imagine talking to one person.

Can I use my phone as a teleprompter?

Yes. Teleprompter apps show scrolling text near the front camera, or on a second device beside the lens. Some follow your voice and scroll as you speak.

Should I record the whole script in one take?

No. Record section by section with pauses, restarting sentences when you stumble. The editor joins the best takes.
