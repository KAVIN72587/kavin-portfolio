Product videos that work as ads

You have paid for a beautiful product video. It runs as an Instagram ad, and the results are disappointing. The video is not bad. It was edited to be watched, not to stop a thumb mid-scroll.

An ad video lives in a feed full of friends' photos, memes and other ads. It has a second or two to earn attention, often with the sound off, and it competes with everything else on the screen. Editing for that is a different craft from editing a brand film. Here is what changes.

01

The hook comes first, always

The first one or two seconds decide whether anyone watches the rest. Open with the most striking moment, not the logo, not a slow pan across a table. A product in use, a before-and-after, a surprising result, a question on screen that the viewer wants answered.

If the original video builds slowly to a reveal, the ad version moves that reveal to the very start.

Watch your ad's first two seconds with the sound off. Would you stop scrolling?

02

Show the product early, and often

Viewers who leave after three seconds should still have seen what you sell. Put the product clearly on screen in the first few seconds and return to it throughout. Close-ups that show texture, size and detail help people imagine owning it. Lifestyle shots work best when the product is still obvious in them.

Show scale too. People cannot pick up a product through a screen, so a hand holding it, a shot next to something familiar, or a quick unboxing answers the question every buyer has: how big is it, really?

03

Design for silent viewing

Many people scroll with the sound off. Burned-in captions for any speech, plus short on-screen text for key points — the benefit, the price, the offer — carry the message without audio. Keep text large, high-contrast and inside the platform's safe area, so buttons and captions from the app do not cover it.

04

Make several lengths and versions

Do not run one cut and hope. Make a six-to-ten-second version, a fifteen-second version and a thirty-second version from the same footage. Try two or three different openings. Ads Manager can show you which performs better, and the answer is often not the one you would have guessed.

Editing variants from footage that is already sorted and graded is much cheaper than a new shoot, and it is where most of the improvement in ad results comes from.

05

End with one clear action

The last few seconds should say exactly what to do: shop now, order on WhatsApp, visit the store, use the code. Show the price or offer if it is good. Keep the call to action on screen long enough to read, and make sure it matches the button and landing page the ad uses.

Make it look like it belongs in the feed

Highly polished ads can look like ads, and people scroll past ads. Some of the best-performing product videos look closer to a creator's reel: handheld, natural light, someone talking to camera about why they use it. Polished and casual styles both have their place; testing both is the only way to know which your audience prefers.

Edit for the scroll, not for the screening room.

Whatever the style, the basics still apply: a strong first second, the product visible early, captions, and one clear action at the end.

Formats to export

  1. 9:16 vertical: for Reels and Stories placements, full screen on a phone.
  2. 4:5 portrait: for feed placements, taking up more screen than square.
  3. 1:1 square: a flexible fallback for placements that need it.

Check Meta's current ad specifications before a campaign — recommended sizes, lengths and safe zones change from time to time. Music must be licensed for commercial and advertising use; a track that worked on an organic post can get an ad rejected.

What to send your editor for an ad

Product: (what it is, price, key benefit in one line) Offer: (discount, code, free delivery — if any) Audience: (who you are targeting) Action: (shop, WhatsApp, visit — and the link) Placements: (Reels, Stories, Feed) Footage: (product close-ups, in-use shots, any customer clips) Brand: (logo, fonts, colours) References: (1–2 ads you liked, with why)

With this, an editor can deliver a set of ready-to-test variants in one go, instead of guessing and revising.

Quick answers

How long should an Instagram ad video be?

Short. Many effective ads are six to fifteen seconds, with longer thirty-second versions for warmer audiences. Make several lengths from the same footage and let the results decide.

Do Instagram and Facebook ads need captions?

Yes. Many people watch with the sound off, so burned-in captions and on-screen text are essential to carry the message without audio.

How much does editing a product ad video cost?

It depends on footage and the number of variants. My short-form edits start at ₹1,500 per cut, and variants made from the same footage cost less because the material is already sorted and graded.
