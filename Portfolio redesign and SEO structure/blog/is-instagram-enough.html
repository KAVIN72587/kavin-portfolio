Is Instagram enough, or do you need a website?

Plenty of businesses in India run entirely on Instagram: home bakers, boutiques, makeup artists, cafés, coaches. Orders come in through DMs and WhatsApp, and it works. So why would they need a website?

Honestly, some do not — yet. If your customers find you through Instagram, buy within a day, and come back because they follow you, a website may not change much. But Instagram has limits that start to cost money as a business grows. Here are the five I see most often.

01

Instagram is rented; a website is owned

Your Instagram account belongs to Meta's platform, under Meta's rules. Accounts get hacked, disabled by mistake, or caught in automated restrictions, and recovery can take weeks with no one to call. Reach changes whenever the algorithm does. Years of followers can become much less valuable overnight.

A website on a domain you own does not disappear because a platform changed its mind. Neither does the customer list you collect through it.

Whatever you decide, save your best photos, captions and customer contacts somewhere outside Instagram.

02

Instagram is weak on Google

Many customers do not start on Instagram. They search Google: "birthday cake Anna Nagar", "bridal makeup artist Chennai price". Instagram profiles rarely rank well for searches like these. A website with a page about each thing you offer, mentioning your area, can show up exactly when someone is ready to buy.

03

Instagram makes people work for basic answers

Prices, menu, delivery areas, how to order, how long it takes. On Instagram, these are buried in highlights, old posts or a pinned comment, and most people simply DM to ask. That works until you are answering the same five questions forty times a day. A single website page with the answers saves you hours and lets serious buyers act without waiting for your reply.

04

Instagram does not look like a business to everyone

For a consumer buying a cake, Instagram is enough proof. For a company booking corporate gifts, a school choosing a vendor, or a family choosing a clinic, a proper website with an address, a phone number and an about page reads as more established. It is not fair, but it is how many buyers decide.

05

Instagram cannot take bookings or payments well

DMs are fine for a few orders a day. Past that, things get lost. A website can show available slots, collect order details in a form, or take payment upfront through a simple payment link. That means fewer no-shows, fewer "is this still available?" chats, and a clearer record of who ordered what.

Use both, for different jobs

Instagram is where people discover you and fall in love with your work. A website is where they check you are real, find the answers they need, and act. The two work best together: every Instagram bio linking to the site, every site page showing your latest posts or linking back to Instagram.

Instagram gets attention. A website turns attention into orders you can count.

You do not need a large site. A single well-designed page with your work, prices, process and a WhatsApp button covers most of what Instagram cannot.

When Instagram really is enough, for now

Hold off on a website if all of these are true:

  1. Almost every customer finds you through Instagram. Not Google, not Maps, not referrals asking for a link.
  2. Orders are simple and fast. One product or service, decided within a day.
  3. You can comfortably handle the DMs. Nobody is waiting hours for a price.
  4. You are not selling to companies or institutions. B2B buyers almost always look for a website.

The moment one of these stops being true — you are missing messages, getting asked "do you have a website?", or losing customers who search on Google — that is the moment a simple site starts paying for itself.

What to put on that first page

If you build one page to sit behind your Instagram bio, give it these, in this order:

  1. What you make or do, in one line. With your area or delivery range.
  2. Your best six to nine pieces of work. The same photos that do well on Instagram, but larger and without the clutter of a feed.
  3. Prices or starting prices. The question that fills your DMs. Answer it once, here.
  4. How ordering works. Lead time, advance payment, delivery or pickup, cancellation.
  5. A WhatsApp button and your Instagram link. So people can ask, or go back and follow.

Quick answers

Can Instagram replace a website for a small business?

For some very small, Instagram-driven businesses it can, for a while. It cannot replace a website for appearing in Google searches, giving detailed information in one place, looking established to business buyers, or protecting you if the account is lost.

What is the cheapest website for an Instagram business?

A single page with your best work, prices, ordering steps and a WhatsApp button. My single-page sites start at ₹9,000, plus roughly ₹4,000 a year for domain and hosting in your own name.

Should my Instagram link go to my website or WhatsApp?

If people mostly want to ask a quick question, WhatsApp. If they need to see prices, menus or portfolios first, the website — with a clear WhatsApp button on it.
