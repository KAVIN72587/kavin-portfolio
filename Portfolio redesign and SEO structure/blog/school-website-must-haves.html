What parents look for on a school website

Admission season brings a wave of calls with the same questions: Is there a seat? What is the process? Is there a bus to our area? A good school website answers those before the phone rings, and makes a strong first impression on parents comparing several schools.

Here is what parents usually look for, the pages that matter most, and how to keep a school site current without depending on a developer for every circular.

01

Admissions, clearly and up front

Admissions is the reason most new parents visit. Put a clear Admissions link in the main menu. Explain which classes are open, the age criteria, the steps (enquiry, form, interaction or test, documents) and the key dates for the year. List the documents needed so parents arrive prepared.

Add a simple enquiry form or a WhatsApp button that goes to the admissions office, and say when someone will respond.

02

Be honest about fees

Parents compare schools on fees, and many leave a site that gives no indication at all. Some schools publish the full structure; others prefer to share it on enquiry. At minimum, explain how fees are structured (term-wise, annual, transport separate) and how to get the current schedule. A vague page that says nothing creates more calls, not fewer.

03

Transport, timings and safety

Bus routes or the areas covered, school timings for each section, and pickup arrangements are among the most searched details. If you have safety measures such as CCTV, attendants on buses, verified staff or controlled entry, describe them plainly. Parents want reassurance, not slogans.

A simple map of the bus areas served answers a question that otherwise comes by phone dozens of times a week.

04

Real photos of real spaces

Classrooms, labs, library, playground, transport and events. Real photos of your campus build far more trust than stock pictures of children who are obviously not your students. Get consent from parents before showing students' faces, and avoid naming children in captions.

05

A notices section that stays current

A notice board with dates for holidays, exams, events and circulars helps current parents and shows prospective ones the school is active. The single biggest problem on school websites is an outdated notice section: a "latest news" page last updated two years ago looks worse than having none.

Choose a setup where office staff can post a notice in a couple of minutes, from a phone if needed.

06

Affiliation, staff and contact details

Show the board affiliation and number, management or trust details, and the principal's message. Include a contact page with the office phone, email, address, a Google Map and office hours. If you have separate admission and accounts contacts, list both. These details answer "is this school legitimate?" for parents who have never visited.

Mobile first, because parents are on phones

Most parents browse school websites on their phones, often in the evening or on WhatsApp links shared in parent groups. Admissions, fees, transport and contact need to be reachable within a tap or two on a small screen, and the site must load quickly on mobile data.

If a parent cannot find the admission steps on their phone in ten seconds, they call, or they move on.

Test the site on an ordinary phone on mobile data. Open it from a WhatsApp link, as parents will, and try to find the admission steps, the bus areas and the phone number.

Pages a school website needs

  1. Home. Who you are, board, classes, and a clear admissions button.
  2. Admissions. Steps, dates, age criteria, documents, enquiry form.
  3. Academics and facilities. Curriculum approach, labs, library, sports, with real photos.
  4. Transport and timings. Areas covered, timings, safety measures.
  5. Notices and events. Kept current by office staff.
  6. Contact. Phone, email, map, office hours.

Optional pages such as a gallery, alumni, careers for teachers or an online fee payment link can be added later. Start with the six above, make sure each answers parents' questions properly, and grow from there.

Before admission season each year, set aside an hour to update the admission dates, fee details, classes open and the photos. Parents notice when last year's dates are still showing, and it is the single most common reason a school website loses their trust.

Quick answers

What should a school website include?

Admissions steps and dates, fee structure or how to get it, transport and timings, facilities with real photos, a current notices section, affiliation details, and clear contact information.

Should a school publish its fees online?

It helps parents compare and reduces repetitive calls. If you prefer not to publish the full schedule, at least explain how fees are structured and how to get the current details.

Can school staff update the website themselves?

Yes, with a content management system such as WordPress set up for simple notice posting. Staff can add notices and photos without a developer.
