A salon website that fills the diary

Most salon clients decide in under a minute: do they do what I want, roughly what will it cost, can I get a slot this week, and is it nearby? A salon website that answers those four questions quickly turns visits into appointments. One that does not just sends people back to Instagram.

Here is what a salon, spa or beauty studio website needs, how to handle bookings without making it complicated, and the common mistakes that cost appointments.

01

Make booking the main button

Every page should have one clear way to book. For many salons, that is a WhatsApp button with a prefilled message: "Hi, I'd like to book a haircut this Saturday." It is familiar, fast and needs no software. Salons with several stylists and busy diaries may prefer an online booking system that shows real availability and sends reminders.

Whichever you choose, put the button at the top of the page on mobile and repeat it after the services and prices. Do not make people hunt for it.

02

Publish a readable price list

Clients compare salons on price. A clear list with starting prices, such as "haircut from ₹…" or "bridal makeup from ₹…", builds trust and filters out people looking for something very different. Prices that vary by hair length or stylist can be shown as ranges.

Put the price list on a proper web page, not as a photo of a printed menu. Images of text are hard to read on phones and invisible to Google.

If you prefer not to publish prices, at least show starting prices for your most popular services.

03

Show real work

Real before-and-after photos of your clients' hair, nails, skin or makeup, taken with consent and in good light, are your strongest proof. Stock photos of models are easy to spot and say nothing about your skill. A gallery organised by service, such as colour, bridal or nails, helps clients find work like what they want.

Add a few photos of the space too: the chairs, the wash area, the waiting area. People want to know what it will feel like to walk in.

04

Introduce the team

Many clients follow a particular stylist. A short team section with each person's photo, specialities and experience helps new clients choose and returning clients book the right person. It also makes the salon feel personal rather than anonymous.

05

Timings, location and parking

Opening hours, the weekly holiday, a Google Map, landmarks and parking details. If you are inside a mall or on an upper floor, say so clearly. Many lost appointments are simply people who could not find the entrance or assumed you were closed.

Keep these details identical on your website, Google Business Profile, Instagram bio and Justdial or similar listings. When one place says you open at 10 and another says 11, clients trust neither, and Google may show the wrong one. Update all of them together whenever timings change, including festival-season hours.

06

Hygiene and products

Clients increasingly care about hygiene and products. If you sterilise tools, use disposable items, or use particular product brands, mention it plainly. For skin and hair treatments, avoid medical-sounding claims and before-and-after promises you cannot guarantee; describe what the treatment involves and let the photos speak.

Instagram brings attention, the website closes the booking

Most salons rely on Instagram for discovery, and that is fine. But a reel does not show prices, timings, location and booking in one place. A simple website does, and it appears in Google searches like "salon near me", where Instagram rarely shows up.

Link your Instagram bio to the booking page, not just the home page.

Keep the website simple and fast. A one-page or three-page site with booking, prices, gallery, team and location is enough for most salons.

A salon site in one scroll

  1. Top. Salon name, area, one line on what you specialise in, Book button.
  2. Services and prices. Grouped by category, with starting prices.
  3. Gallery. Real work, organised by service.
  4. Team. Photos and specialities.
  5. Visit. Hours, map, parking, phone and WhatsApp.

Update the gallery and prices every few months, and especially before wedding season. An outdated price list leads to awkward conversations at the counter, and a stale gallery undersells what your team can do today.

Quick answers

Does a salon need online booking on its website?

Not necessarily. A WhatsApp booking button with a prefilled message works well for many salons. Busy salons with several stylists may benefit from a booking system that shows availability and sends reminders.

Should a salon show prices on its website?

Yes, at least starting prices for popular services. Clients compare on price, and a clear list builds trust and reduces time spent answering the same questions.

What photos should a salon website use?

Real photos of your work, taken with client consent and in good light, plus photos of the salon itself. Avoid stock images of models, which say nothing about your skill.
