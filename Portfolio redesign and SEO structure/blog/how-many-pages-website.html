How many pages does your website actually need?

Quotes for websites are often priced by page, so it is tempting to ask for fewer to save money, or more to look bigger. Neither is the right starting point. The right number is however many questions your customers ask before they buy.

For most small businesses in India — a clinic, a coaching centre, a contractor, a boutique, a consultant — that comes to about five pages. Some need just one. A few need twenty. Here is how to work out which you are.

01

Home: what you do, for whom, and where

In the first screen, a visitor should know what you offer, who it is for, and which area you serve. Below that: a short list of services, a few real photos, some proof (reviews, years in business, well-known clients), and a clear way to contact you. The home page is a summary with signposts, not the place to explain everything.

02

Services: one page each if they are searched separately

This is where page count really comes from. If people search for your services separately — "root canal" and "teeth aligners", or "interior design" and "modular kitchen" — each deserves its own page, because a page about one thing ranks far better for that thing than a list does. If your services are always bought together, one services page is enough.

Rule of thumb: one page per service a customer would type into Google by itself.

03

About: why trust you

Who runs the business, how long you have been doing it, qualifications or registrations that matter in your field, and a real photo of you or your team. People buying a service are buying the person behind it. This page is read far more than business owners expect, usually just before someone gets in touch.

04

Work or gallery: show, do not tell

Photos of finished projects, before-and-afters, a menu, product shots, short case notes. For many trades this is the most visited page after the home page. Real photos taken on a phone beat stock photography every time; visitors can tell the difference.

05

Contact: make it impossible to miss

Phone number, WhatsApp button, address with an embedded map, opening hours, and a short form for people who prefer to write. Put the phone number and WhatsApp in the header of every page as well. A contact page is the destination, but nobody should have to look for it.

When one page is enough

A new business with one main service, a freelancer, a home baker, a tuition class — a single well-built page covering what you do, proof, prices and contact can do everything a five-page site does. It is faster to build, cheaper, and you can add pages later as you grow.

Start with the pages your customers need, not the pages your competitors have.

The mistake is not having too few pages. It is having pages with nothing on them: an empty blog, a gallery with three photos, a "testimonials" page with one quote. Those make a business look smaller, not bigger.

Pages you can skip at the start

These are useful later, but rarely worth paying for on day one.

  1. A blog. Only if you will genuinely post at least monthly. An abandoned blog with two posts from 2024 hurts more than no blog.
  2. A separate testimonials page. Put reviews where people make decisions — on the home and service pages.
  3. FAQ as its own page. Better to answer questions on the page where they come up.
  4. Team bios for every staff member. One good team photo on the About page usually does the job.

Legal pages are the exception: if your site has a contact form or collects any personal details, add a short privacy page. It costs almost nothing and is worth having.

A ten-minute exercise before you ask for quotes

Before talking to any web designer, take a sheet of paper and write down:

  1. The ten questions customers ask most. Check your WhatsApp and call history. Prices, timings, location, "do you do…?" — these are your page contents.
  2. Every service someone might search for by name. Each one that matters to your income is a candidate for its own page.
  3. What you can show. Photos, past projects, certificates, reviews. Pages with nothing to show should wait.
  4. What you want visitors to do. Call, WhatsApp, book, visit. Every page should end by asking for that.

Hand that sheet to your designer. It turns a vague "I need a website" into a clear brief, and it usually makes the page count obvious to both of you.

Quick answers

Is a one-page website bad for SEO?

Not for a business with one main service in one area. It can rank well for that service and your location. It becomes a limit when you offer several distinct services, because one page cannot target all of them well.

How many pages should a small business website have?

Most start with about five: Home, Services (or one page per main service), About, Work or Gallery, and Contact. Add a page whenever there is a service or question customers search for separately.

Does adding more pages cost more?

Usually, yes, because each page needs content, design and testing. My single-page sites start at ₹9,000 and five-page business sites at ₹20,000. The bigger cost is often writing the content, so it helps to start small and add pages when you have something to say.
