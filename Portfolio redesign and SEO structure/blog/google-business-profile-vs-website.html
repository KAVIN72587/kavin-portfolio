Google Business Profile or website: which first?

A Google Business Profile is free and takes an afternoon. A website costs money and takes a week or more. So why would a local business bother with the second one?

Because they answer different questions. Your Business Profile — the listing that appears on Google Maps and on the right of search results — answers "where are you, are you open, and are you any good?". Your website answers "what exactly do you do, how much does it cost, and why should I choose you?".

If you have neither, start with the profile. If you want customers who compare before they call, you need both. Here is why.

01

What the Business Profile does well

It puts you on the map, literally. When someone nearby searches "tailor near me" or "AC service Tambaram", Google shows a small set of local businesses with a map, star ratings, opening hours, photos and a call button. That set of results is where a lot of local customers make their choice.

It also collects reviews, which are the single strongest trust signal for a local business. And it is free, and you control it.

Set it up with your exact business name, address, phone, hours and category. Add real photos. Ask happy customers for reviews.

02

What the Business Profile cannot do

It is a listing, not a sales page. You get a short description, a few posts, photos and a product or service list — all inside Google's layout, next to your competitors. You cannot explain your process, show detailed pricing, answer objections, or tell your story properly.

You also do not own it in the way you own a website. Google decides how it looks, what it shows and what it changes. Profiles get suspended by mistake more often than you would think, and getting one reinstated can take weeks.

03

What a website adds

Room to persuade. A visitor who taps through from Maps wants more detail: your services one by one, prices or price ranges, a gallery of work, who you are, and an easy way to get in touch. A website is where someone who is comparing three options decides you are the one.

It also gives Google more to go on. Your profile's website link, matching name, address and phone on your site, and pages about the services you offer all help Google understand what you do and where.

04

Make them agree with each other

Your business name, address, phone number and hours should be identical on your profile and on your website — same spelling, same format, same number. Mismatches confuse customers and send mixed signals to Google. If you move, change your number or change hours, update both on the same day.

05

Which to do first

If you have nothing online today, set up the Business Profile this week. It is free and starts working immediately. Then build a small website and link it from the profile. A single well-built page is enough to begin with; the point is to have somewhere to send people who want more than a map pin.

Reviews live on Google; proof lives on your site

You cannot copy all your Google reviews onto your website and expect them to look the same, and you should not invent them. What you can do is show proof Google cannot: photos of finished work, names of clients you are allowed to mention, certifications, and a clear explanation of how you work.

The profile gets you shortlisted. The website gets you chosen.

Link to your Google reviews from your website so visitors can read them in the original, and keep asking for new ones. A steady flow of recent reviews matters more than a large pile of old ones.

A fifteen-minute profile check

Open your own listing on a phone and check these, in this order:

  1. Is the business name exactly your name? No added keywords like "Best Dentist Chennai" — Google's guidelines do not allow it, and it can get the profile suspended.
  2. Are the hours right, including holidays? Wrong hours cost you visits and earn angry reviews.
  3. Does the phone number ring a phone someone answers? Use the number you actually pick up, ideally one with WhatsApp.
  4. Is the website link there, and does it work? Tap it. It should open your home page, quickly, on mobile.
  5. Are there photos from the last few months? Add a few new ones every month: the shop, the team, recent work.
  6. Have you replied to recent reviews? Especially the bad ones. Calm, brief, helpful replies are read by future customers.

Quick answers

Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

You can get calls with just a profile, especially for urgent local needs. But for anything people compare before buying — clinics, schools, contractors, caterers, professional services — a website is where they decide, and it protects you if the profile is ever suspended.

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile is free. You may be offered paid ads separately, but you do not need them to have a listing.

Can I make a Google Business Profile without a shop?

Yes, if you serve customers at their location — plumbers, tutors, home bakers delivering locally. You can hide your address and set a service area instead. You need a real way to verify the business.
