Ad creatives that stop the scroll

Many small businesses in Chennai try Instagram and Facebook ads, spend a few thousand rupees and conclude that ads do not work. Often the targeting and budget were fine. The problem was the creative: a cluttered image with too much text, a generic stock photo or a design that looked exactly like every other ad in the feed.

The image or video is the part of an ad people actually see. Here are the design principles that help ad creatives get noticed, understood and clicked.

01

You have about one second

People scroll quickly. Your ad has roughly a second to make someone pause. That means a strong visual, high contrast and a message that can be understood almost instantly.

Ask yourself: if someone saw this for one second with the sound off, would they know what it is and why it matters to them?

02

One clear message

An ad should say one thing: a specific offer, a single product, one benefit or one event. Trying to show your whole range, five offers and all your contact details makes the ad harder to understand and easier to ignore.

If you have several messages, make several ads and test them against each other.

03

Keep text short and readable

Use a short headline on the image, a few words at most, in large, bold type with strong contrast. Put the details in the ad text and headline fields, not on the image. Text on images should be readable on a small phone screen without zooming.

Prices and offers work well on the image when they are the main message: "Flat 30% off this weekend" is clear in an instant.

Check your ad on your own phone before publishing. If you squint to read it, so will everyone else.

04

Real photos often beat stock

Real photos of your products, shop, team and customers tend to feel more trustworthy than polished stock images, especially for local businesses. A clear, bright phone photo of your actual product can outperform a generic studio image.

Make sure photos are well lit, in focus and show the product clearly.

05

Use the right sizes

Design ads in the shapes the platforms use most: square 1080 × 1080 or portrait 1080 × 1350 for feeds, and vertical 1080 × 1920 for stories and reels placements. Ads that do not fit the placement get cropped or shown with awkward borders.

Keep key elements in the centre, so they survive cropping across placements.

06

Brand it, but lightly

Include your logo or brand colours so people remember who the ad was from, but do not make the logo the biggest thing on the image. The offer or product should come first, the brand second.

07

Test variations

Make two or three versions with different images, headlines or offers, and let the platform show them to similar audiences. After a few days, keep the best performer and replace the weakest with a new idea. Small changes, such as a different photo, can make a large difference in results.

The creative is the ad

Targeting decides who might see your ad. The creative decides whether they notice it and act. A simple, clear, well-designed image with one message and a real photo often does more for results than doubling the budget.

One second, one message, one clear reason to stop.

Keep a folder of your best-performing ads. Patterns appear quickly, and they make future ads faster to plan and design.

Ad creative checklist

  1. Hook. Strong visual that stands out in the feed.
  2. Message. One offer, product or benefit per ad.
  3. Text. A few large words on the image, details in the ad copy.
  4. Photo. Real, bright, clear product or shop images.
  5. Sizes. Feed, portrait and story versions.
  6. Testing. Two or three variations, keep the winner.

Brief your designer with the goal of the ad, the audience and the single message. It leads to stronger creatives than asking for "an ad for our shop".

Also plan where people land after clicking. An eye-catching ad that sends people to a confusing page or an unanswered WhatsApp chat wastes the click you paid for. Make sure the landing page, form or chat continues the same message and offer, so the person who tapped feels they are in the right place straight away.

Refresh creatives regularly. Even a strong ad gets tired after people have seen it several times, and results slowly drop. Plan a new image or headline every couple of weeks for longer campaigns.

Quick answers

What makes a good Facebook or Instagram ad image?

A strong visual, one clear message, short readable text, real photos and the right size for the placement.

How much text should be on an ad image?

As little as possible: a short headline or offer in large type. Put details in the ad copy fields instead.

What size should Instagram ad creatives be?

Square 1080 × 1080 or portrait 1080 × 1350 for feeds, and 1080 × 1920 for stories and reels placements.
