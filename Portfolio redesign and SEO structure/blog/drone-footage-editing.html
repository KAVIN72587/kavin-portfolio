Drone shots: less is more

A sweeping aerial shot over a property, a factory or a wedding venue instantly makes a video feel bigger. It is also one of the most overused effects in business videos. A minute of drone footage in a two-minute film quickly feels like a screensaver.

Here is what drone footage is good for, how much to use, how editors make it fit with ground footage, and the permissions to think about before anyone flies. Drone rules in India are set by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation and change from time to time, so check the current rules for your location.

01

What drone shots are for

Drone footage is best at establishing: showing where something is, how big it is and what surrounds it. A real estate project in relation to the main road and the lake. A factory's scale. A wedding venue at sunset. A resort's beach frontage. It answers the question "what is this place like?" in seconds.

It is weaker at telling stories about people. Faces, hands, products and emotions are ground-level subjects.

02

How much to use

Usually a few well-chosen shots: one to open, one or two to transition between sections, and perhaps one to close. In a 60 to 90 second business video, ten to fifteen seconds of aerial footage is often plenty. Real estate and resort videos can use more, because location is the product, but even then, intercut with interiors and people.

If the drone shot does not tell the viewer something new, cut it, however beautiful it looks.

03

Shoot with purpose

The best drone shots are planned: a slow reveal from behind a building, a gentle push towards the entrance, a rising shot that shows the surroundings, a top-down view of a layout. Smooth, slow movement looks professional. Fast, jerky movement and constant circling look amateur. Golden hour, the hour after sunrise and before sunset, makes almost any location look better.

04

Matching drone and ground footage

Drone cameras record colour and contrast differently from the main camera or phone. Without colour correction, aerial shots can look like they belong to a different video: more saturated, a different white balance or a different level of sharpness. Matching them to the ground footage is part of a proper edit, and it makes the whole film feel consistent.

05

Stabilisation and speed

Modern drones are well stabilised, but wind still causes wobbles. Editors can stabilise slightly in post and often slow aerial shots down a little to make them feel smoother. This works best when the drone recorded at a higher frame rate. Ask your pilot to record at a higher frame rate if slow-motion aerials are planned.

06

Permissions and safety

In India, drone operations are regulated. Depending on the drone's weight category and the purpose, registration, a remote pilot certificate and permission for the airspace may be required. Some areas, such as near airports and certain sensitive locations, are restricted. Many venues and gated communities have their own rules. Hire a pilot who knows and follows the current regulations; the responsibility should be theirs, and they should be able to explain what applies.

07

Privacy and consent

Drones see over walls. Avoid filming into neighbours' homes, people who have not agreed to be filmed, and private property you do not have permission for. At events, let guests know a drone will be flying. For real estate, frame shots to show the project and its surroundings, not identifiable people in nearby homes.

Aerial is seasoning, not the meal

The most memorable business videos use drone footage the way a good meal uses spice: enough to lift it, never so much that it overpowers. A single breathtaking reveal at the right moment does more than two minutes of circling overhead.

Use the drone to set the scene, then come down to the people.

When planning a shoot, list what the drone must show that the ground camera cannot. If the list is short, so is the drone time, and so is the cost.

Drone shot list for a business video

  1. Opening reveal. Slow rise or push showing the location.
  2. Context. The site in relation to roads, landmarks or nature.
  3. Layout. A top-down view if the plan matters.
  4. Transition. One aerial between indoor sections.
  5. Closing. A slow pull-away at golden hour.

Five purposeful shots, each held for ten to fifteen seconds of usable footage, give an editor everything needed for a typical business video.

Quick answers

How much drone footage should a business video have?

Usually a few purposeful shots: an opening, one or two transitions and a closing. In a 60 to 90 second video, around ten to fifteen seconds of aerial footage is often enough.

Do I need permission to fly a drone for a business video in India?

Often yes, depending on the drone's category, the location and the purpose. Rules are set by the DGCA and change over time. Hire a pilot who follows the current regulations.

Why does drone footage look different from the rest of my video?

Drone cameras record colour and contrast differently. Colour correction in the edit matches aerial shots to the ground footage so the video looks consistent.
