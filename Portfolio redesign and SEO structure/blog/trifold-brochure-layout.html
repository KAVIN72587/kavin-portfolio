Trifold brochures, panel by panel

The trifold brochure, an A4 sheet folded into three, is the most common brochure format in India. Clinics, schools, coaching centres, restaurants and service businesses all use it. Yet many trifolds are laid out as if they were a flat poster, with the contact details on the wrong panel and the main message hidden inside a flap.

A trifold is read in a specific order as it is unfolded. Planning for that order is the difference between a brochure that tells a clear story and one that confuses. Here is how the panels work and what to put on each.

01

How the panels are arranged

A trifold has six panels: three on the outside and three on the inside. In the most common roll fold, the outside has the front cover on the right, the back panel in the middle and the inside flap on the left. The inside spreads across three panels.

When someone picks up the brochure, they see the front cover. When they open it, they see the inside flap first, then the full inside spread. The back panel is often seen last, when they turn the brochure over.

02

Front cover: attract

The front cover has one job: make the right person want to open it. Use your logo, a strong headline that speaks to the reader's need and a clear image. Avoid listing everything you do here.

For a clinic, that might be "Gentle dental care for the whole family" with a warm photo. For a coaching centre, "NEET preparation that builds confidence".

03

Inside flap: introduce

The inside flap is the first panel seen after opening. It is a good place for a short introduction, a key benefit, an offer or a quick summary of why you are different. It bridges the cover and the detailed inside.

04

Inside spread: explain

The three inside panels can be read together as one spread. This is where you explain your services, products, process or courses, with headings, short paragraphs, photos and perhaps a simple list or table.

You can treat the spread as three separate columns or as one wide layout. Either way, keep a clear reading flow from left to right and use headings so people can scan.

The inside flap and the left inside panel are seen together briefly as the brochure opens. Make sure they do not clash.

05

Back panel: act

The back panel usually holds contact details: address, phone, WhatsApp, email, website, a map or QR code and opening hours. It is also the panel visible when the brochure is lying face down on a counter or posted without an envelope, so it should look tidy and branded.

Some businesses put testimonials or a short "why choose us" section here as well.

06

Panel widths are not equal

For a roll fold to close neatly, the panel that folds inside must be slightly narrower, usually by 2 to 3 mm, than the other two. If all panels are exactly one third of the sheet, the inner flap buckles at the fold.

Your printer can confirm exact panel widths for their folding machine. Your designer should build the layout on those measurements, with bleed around the outside and safe margins away from each fold.

07

Common mistakes

Putting the contact details on the inside flap, where they get lost. Designing the front cover on the left panel, so it ends up inside. Placing important text across a fold. Crowding the inside with too much text in a small font. Each of these is avoided by planning the fold before designing.

Plan the fold, then the design

A trifold brochure tells a story in a fixed order: attract on the cover, introduce on the flap, explain inside, invite action on the back. When the content follows that order, the brochure feels natural to read and guides people towards contacting you.

Fold a paper mockup and read it like a customer before you approve it.

A quick test: print the draft, fold it and hand it to someone who does not know your business. Watch where they look first and what they miss.

Trifold panel plan

  1. Front cover. Logo, headline, image. Make people open it.
  2. Inside flap. Short intro, key benefit or offer.
  3. Inside spread. Services, details, photos, process.
  4. Back panel. Contact details, map, QR code, hours.

Write your content panel by panel before design starts. It makes the designer's job faster and the brochure clearer.

If you have too much content for six panels, that is a sign to either cut it down or choose a different format, such as a bifold with an insert or a short multi-page booklet. Squeezing everything into a trifold with tiny text rarely works.

Quick answers

What goes on the front of a trifold brochure?

Your logo, a clear headline aimed at your customer and a strong image. Its job is to make people open the brochure.

Where should contact details go on a trifold brochure?

Usually on the back panel, which is the middle panel of the outside. Some businesses repeat key contact details inside as well.

Are all panels of a trifold brochure the same width?

No. The panel that folds inside is usually 2 to 3 mm narrower so the brochure closes neatly. Confirm exact widths with your printer.
