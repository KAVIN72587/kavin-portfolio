Testimonial videos that don't feel scripted

A real customer saying, in their own words, what changed after working with you is one of the most persuasive things a small business can show. A stiff customer reading a script is one of the least. The difference is almost entirely in how you ask.

Here is how to choose the right customers, which questions draw out natural answers, how to film it simply, and how the edit turns twenty minutes of conversation into a minute that works on your website and social media.

01

Choose customers with a story

The best testimonial is not from your happiest customer; it is from the customer with the clearest before-and-after. Someone who had a specific problem, tried something else, came to you and saw a specific result. That story is what prospective customers recognise in themselves.

Ask people who are comfortable talking. Some lovely customers freeze on camera, and that is fine; a written review from them works better.

02

Never give them a script

Scripts make people sound like adverts. Instead, have a conversation. Ask open questions and let them answer in their own words, in Tamil, English or a mix of both, whatever is natural. Ask them to include your question in their answer, such as "Before I found them, I was…", so each answer makes sense on its own without your voice.

03

Five questions that work

What was the problem before you came to us? What else had you tried? Why did you choose us? What happened, and what is different now? What would you tell someone in your situation who is deciding? Follow up with "Can you give me an example?" whenever an answer is vague. The example is usually the line that ends up in the video.

Let silences sit for a moment. People often add their best line after a pause.

04

A simple filming setup

A phone on a tripod, at eye level, horizontal for the website and with room to crop vertical for reels. Face a window for soft light. A lapel mic clipped to their shirt, a hand's width from the chin. A quiet room with soft furnishings. Have the person look at you, slightly off camera, not into the lens.

Film a few seconds of them in context too: using the product, at their shop, walking in. These shots cover edits and make the video feel less like a talking head.

05

Get consent in writing

Before filming, explain where the video will be used: website, Instagram, ads. Get a short written consent, even a WhatsApp message saying they agree, and keep it. If they later ask for it to be taken down, take it down. Never present an actor or staff member as a customer. It is misleading, and viewers usually notice.

06

What the edit does

Twenty minutes of conversation becomes 45 to 60 seconds: the problem, the turning point, the result, the recommendation. The editor removes hesitations without making the person sound unnatural, covers jump cuts with the context shots, adds subtitles for silent viewing and lower-thirds with their name and business. A shorter 15-second cut of the strongest line makes a good reel or ad.

Specific beats enthusiastic

"They're amazing, highly recommend" is pleasant and forgettable. "Our enquiries doubled in the first month after the new site went live" or "The wedding film made my grandmother cry" is memorable because it is specific.

One specific result is worth ten adjectives.

When editing, look for the concrete details: a number, a moment, a comparison. Build the video around those, and let the general praise go.

Testimonial shoot checklist

  1. Consent. Where it will be used, agreed in writing.
  2. Setup. Tripod, window light, lapel mic, quiet room.
  3. Questions. Open, about the before and after, with follow-ups for examples.
  4. Context shots. Thirty seconds of them using the product or at their place.
  5. Name and role. Spelled correctly for the on-screen title.

Plan for about thirty minutes per customer, including setup. Most of that time is putting them at ease. The actual answers you use will come from four or five minutes of the conversation, usually towards the end, once they have stopped thinking about the camera.

If you have several happy customers, film three or four on the same day in the same setup. The videos will look consistent as a set, and the editing cost per video usually drops.

Quick answers

How long should a testimonial video be?

Around 45 to 60 seconds for the main version, plus a 15-second cut of the strongest line for reels and ads. Longer versions can work on a dedicated case-study page.

Should customers read a script for a testimonial?

No. Scripts make people sound like adverts. Ask open questions and let them answer naturally, then shape the answers in the edit.

Can I film a testimonial video on a phone?

Yes. Use a tripod, face the person towards a window, clip a lapel mic close to their chin, and film in a quiet room. Good sound matters more than the camera.
