The design brief that saves a revision round

Most design jobs start with a WhatsApp message like "Need a flyer for our offer, urgent." The designer makes something reasonable, then the real requirements appear one by one: it needs to be A5, in Tamil and English, with the new address, and the offer has changed. Each discovery costs time and often a revision round.

A simple brief, sent once at the start, avoids almost all of that. It works for any design job, from a single social post to a brochure or product label. Here is what to include, with examples.

01

What is being designed

Be precise about the item and its format. Not just "a flyer", but "an A5 flyer, printed both sides, 2,000 copies". Not just "posts", but "three Instagram feed posts and one story".

If it will be printed, mention the size, sides, paper if known and printer details. If it is digital, mention the platforms.

02

The goal

What should happen after someone sees it? Call to book, visit the shop, order on WhatsApp, attend an event, remember your brand? One clear goal helps the designer decide what to emphasise.

Example: "We want new families in Velachery to call and book a free dental check-up."

03

The audience

Who is this for? Age, location, language, what they care about. A flyer for college students and one for retired professionals should look and read very differently, even for the same business.

04

The content

Send all the text, final and proofread, in one document: headline, details, offer, terms, contact information. Include every logo, photo and product image in the best quality you have.

If you need help writing the text, say so. Copywriting is a separate task, and planning for it avoids delays.

Final text before design is the single biggest time saver. Changing text after layout often means redesigning the layout.

05

Brand and style

Attach your logo files and mention your brand colours and fonts, or share your brand kit if you have one. Add two or three examples of designs you like, from any business, with a line on what you like about each. Mention anything to avoid.

06

Practical details

Deadline, including when printing must start. Budget, or at least a range. Who will give feedback and approve the final design. Any mandatory elements, such as logos of partners, certifications or legal text.

07

How long a brief should be

One page is enough for most jobs. The point is not paperwork but completeness. A brief with short, clear answers is far more useful than a long one with vague ones. If writing feels difficult, a 15-minute call where the designer asks these questions works just as well.

A brief is the cheapest part of any design job

Ten minutes spent writing a brief can save days of back and forth and at least one revision round. It also makes quotes more accurate, because the designer knows exactly what is involved before giving a price.

Everything the designer needs, in one message, before they start.

Save your completed brief. The next time you need a similar job, most of it can be reused with small changes.

Copy-and-paste brief template

Item: what, size, sides, quantity Print or digital: printer / platforms Goal: what should people do after seeing it Audience: who, where, language Content: final text attached (yes / need help) Images: logos and photos attached Brand: colours, fonts, brand kit link Likes: examples and why Avoid: colours, styles, words Deadline: design by / print by Budget: range Approval: who signs off

Fill this in for every new design job. Even short answers make a big difference.

If you work with the same designer regularly, you will find the brief gets shorter over time. Brand, style and approval details stay the same, so each new job only needs the item, goal, content and deadline. That is when design work starts to feel quick and easy for both sides.

A good brief also protects your budget. When the designer can see the full job at the start, the quote reflects the real work, and there are fewer surprise charges later for things that were never mentioned. It is one of the simplest ways to keep a project on time and on price.

If you are unsure about any section, leave a note rather than guessing. An honest gap is easier to fill than a wrong answer.

Quick answers

What should a graphic design brief include?

The item and format, the goal, the audience, final content and images, brand guidelines, examples you like, deadline, budget and who approves the design.

How long should a design brief be?

One page is usually enough. Clear, complete answers matter more than length.

Do I need to write the text for my flyer or brochure?

Ideally yes, or tell the designer you need copywriting help. Final text before design starts saves the most time and money.
