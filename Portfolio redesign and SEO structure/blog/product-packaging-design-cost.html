What packaging really costs a small brand

New food, cosmetics and home product brands often budget carefully for ingredients and production, then get surprised by packaging. The design quote seems reasonable, but then come printing minimums, dies, samples, label changes and reprints. Knowing the full picture in advance makes launch planning much easier.

Here is what packaging design typically costs in India, what changes the price, and the other packaging costs a small brand should plan for.

01

Design fees are usually per SKU

Packaging design is usually priced per SKU, meaning each unique product variant. A masala brand with three flavours in one box style has three SKUs. Once the first design is done, related variants often cost less, because the layout and branding are already set.

My packaging design starts at ₹8,000 per SKU, which includes dieline-accurate artwork, bilingual label copy fitting and coordination with your printer. Complex structures, illustrations or large ranges are quoted after the brief.

02

What affects the design price

The main factors are the number of panels and the amount of information, whether you need custom illustrations or photography, whether the brand identity already exists or must be created, the number of languages, and how many variants share the design.

A simple label for a single jar is quicker than a printed box with six panels, nutrition tables, regulatory text in two languages and a window cutout.

03

Printing is often the bigger cost

For many small brands, printing costs more than design. Prices depend on the packaging type, material, size, number of colours, finishes like lamination, foil or spot UV, and especially quantity.

Offset and gravure printing have setup costs, so the price per piece drops sharply at larger quantities. Digital printing suits smaller runs but costs more per piece. Ask printers for quotes at several quantities to see where the price breaks are.

Custom box shapes need a new cutting die, which is a one-time cost. Standard box styles from your printer avoid it.

04

Minimum order quantities

Printers often have minimum order quantities, especially for printed pouches and cartons. A new brand may have to order more packaging than it can sell quickly, which ties up cash and storage space.

Options include starting with printed labels on plain boxes or pouches, choosing digital printing for the first run, or using one shared box design with variant stickers until sales grow.

05

Costs new brands forget

Samples and proofs before the main print run. Barcodes, which need registration. Photography of the product for the pack and for online listings. Changes to regulatory text if rules or ingredients change. Shipping boxes and inserts for online orders. Reprints when a detail turns out to be wrong.

Building a small contingency into the packaging budget, perhaps 10 to 15 percent, avoids stress when one of these appears.

06

How to budget sensibly

Start with the design for your hero product, test it with a small print run, then extend to other variants. Get quotes from two or three printers before the design is final, because their capabilities may affect the design. Keep the design flexible enough that changing printers later does not require starting again.

Plan design and printing together

Packaging design and printing are closely linked. Choosing materials, sizes and finishes early, with your printer involved, means the design is made for how the pack will actually be produced. That avoids redesigns, surprise costs and delays just before launch.

Get the printing quote before you fall in love with the design.

Keep all final artwork files, dielines and colour references together. Reorders and new variants are faster and cheaper when everything is organised.

Packaging budget checklist

  1. Design. Fee per SKU, plus any illustration or photography.
  2. Printing. Quotes at several quantities, with finishes.
  3. Setup. Dies, plates or cylinders for custom or large runs.
  4. Samples. Proofs and physical samples before the main run.
  5. Extras. Barcodes, shipping boxes, inserts, labels.
  6. Contingency. 10–15% for changes and surprises.

Write the budget per piece as well as in total. It helps when setting your product's selling price.

Finally, remember that packaging is part of your marketing, not only a container. A pack that looks trustworthy on a shelf or in an online listing can justify a better price and win repeat buyers. When you compare costs, compare them against what the packaging needs to achieve for your product, not just against the cheapest option available.

Quick answers

How much does packaging design cost in India?

Packaging design for small brands typically starts from around ₹8,000 per SKU for straightforward work, with more complex boxes, illustration or large ranges costing more.

Why is packaging printing so expensive for small quantities?

Offset and gravure printing have setup costs that are spread across the order. Small quantities carry a higher cost per piece. Digital printing can be better for short runs.

Can I start with labels instead of printed boxes?

Yes. Printed labels on plain boxes, jars or pouches are a common way for new brands to launch with lower minimum quantities and costs.
