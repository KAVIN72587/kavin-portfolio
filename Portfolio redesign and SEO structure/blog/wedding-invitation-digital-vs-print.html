Digital or printed wedding invitations?

Wedding invitations in Tamil Nadu used to mean one thing: a printed card, often heavy, gold-printed and personally delivered with sweets. Today, many families also send e-invites on WhatsApp, video invitations on Instagram and digital save-the-dates months in advance. Some skip print entirely; others print fewer, more special cards for elders and close family.

Here is how digital and printed wedding invitations compare, and how to design each so it feels personal and works well.

01

Printed invitations

Printed cards carry tradition and respect. For elders, relatives and family friends, receiving a card in person, often with a visit, remains an important gesture. A well-designed card is also kept as a memento.

Printed cards cost more per piece, especially with premium paper, foil, laser cuts, boxes or inserts. They take longer to design, proof and print, and they need to be delivered or posted.

02

Digital e-invites

E-invites are images or PDFs shared on WhatsApp, email or social media. They reach many people instantly, cost much less and are easy to update with venue maps, timings and livestream links. They are ideal for friends, colleagues and guests in other cities or countries.

A good e-invite is designed for phone screens: vertical or square format, large readable text and essential details clearly visible without zooming.

Include a Google Maps link and a contact number in the message along with the e-invite image, so guests can tap rather than type.

03

Video invitations

Video invitations, from simple animated cards to short films with pre-wedding footage, music and couple names, have become popular, especially for sharing on Instagram and WhatsApp status. They feel personal and festive.

Keep videos short, ideally under a minute, with key details shown clearly on screen at the end. Use licensed or original music to avoid copyright problems on social platforms.

04

Using both

Many families now use a mix: a small number of printed cards for elders, close family and important guests, and e-invites or video invitations for everyone else. This keeps tradition where it matters most and reduces cost and effort elsewhere.

Design them as a set, with the same colours, motifs and typography, so the invitation feels consistent whichever version guests receive.

05

Getting details right

Whether digital or printed, check every detail carefully: names and their spellings, parents' and grandparents' names, dates, muhurtham timings, venue names, addresses and contact numbers. Tamil text should be checked by a fluent reader. Mistakes on wedding invitations are noticed and remembered.

06

Timeline

For printed cards, start design two to three months before the wedding, allowing time for proofs, printing and distribution. E-invites can be prepared in a few days but are best sent several weeks ahead, with a reminder closer to the date. Save-the-dates can go out even earlier.

07

Budget tips

Printing a smaller number of premium cards often feels more special than many ordinary ones. Use digital invitations for the larger guest list. Ask your designer for the e-invite as part of the same project, since the artwork already exists, which is usually more economical than ordering separately.

Tradition where it matters, convenience everywhere else

There is no single right choice. Printed cards honour tradition and make a lasting impression on close family. Digital and video invitations reach everyone quickly and affordably. Used together, with a consistent design, they give you the best of both.

Print for the people who'll keep it. Send digital to everyone else.

Keep a final digital copy of your printed card. It becomes a lovely keepsake and is useful for anniversary posts years later.

Invitation planning checklist

  1. Guest list. Split into printed and digital recipients.
  2. Design set. Printed card, e-invite and optional video, matching.
  3. Details. Names, dates, timings, venue checked twice.
  4. Language. Tamil and English text proofread by fluent readers.
  5. Timeline. Print design 2–3 months ahead, e-invites weeks ahead.
  6. Extras. Maps link, contact number, livestream link.

Ask for printed proofs before the full print run, especially for foil, special papers or die-cut cards.

Think about the other printed pieces that go with a wedding too: welcome boards, seating charts, menu cards, thank-you tags and return gift labels. Designing them alongside the invitation keeps the whole celebration visually consistent, and it is usually quicker and more affordable to plan them together than to rush each one in the final week before the wedding.

Most of all, make the invitation feel like you. Personal touches, such as a family motif, a meaningful colour or a short line in your own words, matter more than expensive finishes.

Quick answers

Are digital wedding invitations acceptable?

Yes, increasingly so, especially for friends, colleagues and distant guests. Many families still send printed cards to elders and close relatives.

How early should wedding invitations be sent?

Printed cards are usually distributed several weeks before the wedding. Start design two to three months ahead to allow for printing.

What size should an e-invite be?

A vertical or square format sized for phone screens, such as 1080 × 1920 or 1080 × 1350 pixels, with large readable text.
