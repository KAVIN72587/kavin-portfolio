Does your business need a blog?

Many small business websites have a blog section with three posts, the last one from two years ago. It was started with good intentions and abandoned when work got busy. Others have a blog that quietly brings in a steady stream of enquiries from Google every month. The difference is not writing talent. It is what they write about and why.

Here is when a blog is genuinely useful for a small business, what to write about, how often to post, how to measure whether it is working, and when your time would be better spent on something else.

01

What a blog actually does

A business blog is useful in three main ways. It answers questions customers search for, bringing visitors from Google who would never have found your service pages. It builds trust, by showing expertise before someone contacts you. And it gives you useful content to share in WhatsApp replies, social media and emails.

02

When it is worth it

A blog is most useful when customers research before buying: website design, video production, renovation, education, healthcare, finance, legal and B2B services. If people search questions like "how much does…", "how long does…", "which is better…", there is an audience for helpful articles.

It is less useful for impulse or purely local walk-in businesses, where a strong Google Business Profile and good photos matter more.

03

Write answers, not news

The posts that bring visitors are answers to real questions customers ask. Not "We attended an exhibition" or "Happy Diwali", but "How much does a wedding film cost in Chennai?" or "Which paint finish is easiest to clean?" Keep a list of questions customers ask you; each one is a potential post.

If a customer has asked it, someone else is searching for it.

04

Be useful and honest

Good business posts are practical, specific and honest, including about prices, timelines and when you are not the right fit. Generic articles that could be on any website rarely rank or persuade. Your own experience and local detail are what make a post worth reading.

05

How often to post

Consistency matters more than frequency. Two genuinely useful posts a month, every month, will do more over a year than a burst of daily posts followed by silence. Publishing large numbers of thin or very similar posts can work against you, because search engines look for helpful content written for people, not volume.

06

Link posts to services

Each post should link to the relevant service or pricing page, and end with a clear next step. A visitor who finds your post about logo file formats is a potential logo client. Make it easy for them to see what you offer and get in touch.

07

Measure what matters

After six months, check in Search Console which posts get impressions and clicks, and in Analytics which posts lead to enquiries. Update the posts that work, improve those that almost work, and stop writing the kind that bring nothing. Results take time; most posts take weeks or months to find their audience.

08

If writing is not your thing

You do not have to write every post yourself to keep it authentic. Record yourself answering a customer question on your phone for five minutes, then have it transcribed and edited into an article. Or answer the question in a short video and publish the video with a written summary. The expertise and the examples should be yours; the polishing can be done by someone else.

If you will not keep it going, do not start

A blog section with three posts from years ago signals that the business is not very active. If you cannot commit to a steady rhythm, it may be better to add a well-written FAQ section to your service pages instead.

One useful answer a fortnight beats a burst of posts and two years of silence.

If you do start, plan the first ten topics from real customer questions before writing the first post. A list removes the hardest part: deciding what to write next.

Is a blog right for you?

  1. Customers research before buying? A blog can help.
  2. Customers ask the same questions? Each one is a post.
  3. Time for two posts a month? Enough to build results.
  4. Mostly walk-in, impulse purchases? Focus on Google Business Profile first.
  5. No time at all? Add FAQs to service pages instead.

Whatever you decide, the same principle applies: answer real questions honestly, in your own words, where customers can find them.

Quick answers

Is blogging worth it for a small business?

It can be, especially for businesses whose customers research before buying. Useful, honest posts answering real questions can bring steady visitors from search.

How often should a small business blog?

Consistency matters more than frequency. Two useful posts a month, kept up over time, is a realistic and effective rhythm for most small businesses.

What should a business blog write about?

Answers to the questions customers actually ask: prices, timelines, comparisons, how-to guides and common mistakes, with honest, local detail.
